75% Replaced. 0% Consent.

In 1994, 19.7 million South Africans voted under a constitution that guaranteed power-sharing, protected their languages, and described itself as a "solemn pact." Within two years, every word of that document was replaced. The party that wrote the replacement achieved 100% of its objectives. Every other party achieved zero. This is the forensic record of what was agreed, what was deleted, and the R250 billion trail that followed.

What This Investigation Does

This page places the constitution South Africans endorsed in 1994 next to the constitution they live under in 2026 and measures, provision by provision, what changed. It documents which protections were deleted, which new powers were added, which political parties won and lost, and which demographic groups benefited or were harmed by the changes. It traces the financial consequences of three constitutional provisions that did not exist in the original text, from their insertion in 1996 through to R250 billion in documented state capture three decades later. It maps every change against the ANC-SACP alliance's own published revolutionary programme to test whether the pattern is coincidental or architectural.

Every claim is sourced to the constitutional texts themselves, official party documents, court judgments, parliamentary records, or the IRR Index of Race Law. Nothing is fabricated. The reader is given the evidence and invited to draw their own conclusions.

Section 01

What the People Actually Voted For

There is a question that has never been properly asked in South African public life, and it is this: what exactly did the people of South Africa vote for in 1994?

Not who they voted for. What they voted under.

On 27 April 1994, some 19.7 million citizens walked to polling stations across the country. They cast their ballots under the protection of the Interim Constitution, Act 200 of 1993, a document they had been told represented a negotiated settlement between all major parties. Its Preamble called the deal a "solemn pact." Its Schedule 4 contained 34 Constitutional Principles that no parliament, no president, and no party could amend or repeal. That was the architecture. That was the guarantee.

The document contained 14 distinct protections that made this settlement different from a simple handover of power. Mandatory power-sharing through a Government of National Unity. A non-diminution clause, repeated three times in a single section, guaranteeing that the status of every language in use at the time of transition could not be reduced. An individual right to be addressed by any government department in the official language of your choice. Property rights that limited expropriation to "public purposes only," with compensation either agreed or set by a court. A limitation clause with a hard floor: no government action could "negate the essential content" of any right. A Volkstaat Council mandated to investigate self-determination for communities sharing a cultural and language heritage. And the 34 Principles themselves, locked behind Section 74(1), described in the ANC's own 2017 Strategy and Tactics document as "sunset clauses" and "reconciliatory assurances to the white community."

The compromises contained in the 'sunset clauses', security operations and reconciliatory assurances to the white community formed part of this arsenal. This tactical approach afforded the liberation movement the possibility to capture a political beach-head, and use it to advance towards the ultimate ideal. In that sense, it was profoundly revolutionary.

ANC Strategy & Tactics 2017, Paragraph 70

The word is "arsenal." Not "agreement." Not "covenant." Arsenal. The protections that 19.7 million people relied upon were, in the ANC's own official language, weapons in a revolutionary strategy.

The 1993 Constitution no longer exists. It was repealed in its entirety on 4 February 1997. The document that replaced it was written by a Constitutional Assembly in which the ANC held 63.7% of the seats, chaired by Cyril Ramaphosa, with the NP's Leon Wessels as his deputy. It was adopted on 8 May 1996 by a vote of 421 to 2, with 10 abstentions. The two no votes came from the African Christian Democratic Party. The Inkatha Freedom Party boycotted the entire process.

The following morning, F.W. de Klerk announced the NP's withdrawal from the Government of National Unity, citing the absence of power-sharing provisions in the new text. The ANC accepted "without reservations."

Section 02

The Numbers They Cannot Dispute

The forensic comparison of the two constitutional texts produces numbers that do not require interpretation. They speak for themselves.

The language provisions shrank by 58.8%. Every word removed was a protective clause. The phrase "shall not be diminished" appeared three times in the 1993 text. It appears zero times in the 1996 text. The individual right to be addressed in the language of your choice was deleted. "Equal use and enjoyment" was replaced with "equitably," a word that sounds similar but means something quite different: equitably allows differential treatment deemed fair, while equally does not. The word "expense" was added as a qualifier for government language services, providing a permanent administrative excuse to discontinue them.

The property clause expanded by 244%. Every additional word serves the state, not the property holder. "Public purposes only" became "public purpose or in the public interest," with land reform explicitly defined as falling within the public interest. A new subsection, Section 25(8), states that nothing in the property clause may "impede the state from taking legislative and other measures to achieve land, water and related reform." A right that contains a clause preventing itself from being enforced is, structurally, not a right at all.

The self-determination provisions lost 83.3% of their text. The Volkstaat Council, a 20-member constitutional body with a funded mandate and a dedicated chapter, was replaced by Section 235: a single sentence of triple-hedged permission, dependent on "national legislation" that has not been enacted in thirty years.

The Asymmetry Index

For every area where the text was reduced, the reduction removed protections. For every area where the text was expanded, the expansion added state powers. There is not a single provision in the 1996 Constitution that is longer than its 1993 equivalent and provides greater protection for the same right. The correlation is 100%.

Section 03

Whose Interests Does This Constitution Serve?

The Constitutional Assembly was a 490-member body. The ANC held 312 seats. The two-thirds threshold for adoption was 327 votes, meaning the ANC needed only 15 votes from any other party. The final vote confirmed this: 421 to 2, with 10 abstentions.

The ANC presented 14 identifiable objectives during the drafting process. It achieved all 14. The NP presented 12 objectives. It achieved zero full wins. The IFP boycotted. The Freedom Front achieved one partial victory, then watched it repealed. One party achieved 100% of its objectives. Every other party achieved 0%. In the recorded history of constitutional drafting, this outcome has no parallel. It is the statistical signature of a replacement, not a negotiation.

The downstream beneficiary map follows the constitutional architecture precisely. BEE transactions since 1996 exceed R600 billion. Government procurement channelled through Section 217, a provision that did not exist in the 1993 text, totals approximately R1.8 trillion per year. The Zondo Commission documented R250 billion in state capture flowing through the procurement framework the 1996 Constitution created.

Investigation Verdict
The constitution South Africa operates under in 2026 is not the constitution that was agreed to in 1993. It is a different document, written by a different body, serving a different purpose, benefiting a different set of people. The negotiated deal is 100% gone.
