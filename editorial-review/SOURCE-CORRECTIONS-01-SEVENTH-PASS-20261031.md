## Documentary question: 1979 philanthropic start versus a 1980 visit

The original visible hero, body and timeline implied **80 students were already funded in 1979**. There are conflicting time references in the funder's own record; a proposed award capacity is not a certified disbursement count.

1. **Foundation primary institutional retrospective:** OSF's historical timeline and South African project summary date Soros's earliest South African university scholarship initiative to **1979**. The March 2025 30-year South Africa institutional report similarly dates the Karl Popper Bursary Fund to 1979 and describes support for 80 students. These are organisational reports, not payment registers: https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/who-we-are/our-history and https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/newsroom/open-society-foundations-south-africa .
2. **Founder first-person primary account, 1 November 2016:** George Soros explicitly recalls visiting South Africa **in 1980** and subsequently agreeing to cover living costs for **up to 80** Black University of Cape Town students. That attests to intended capacity, not a count of paid 1979 bursaries: https://www.opensocietyfoundations.org/voices/scholarships-launched-open-society-foundations .
3. **Independent secondary historical context:** Reference biography https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/social-sciences-and-law/business-leaders/george-soros identifies early university grant activity but does not independently reconcile exact payment dates or ledger count.

**Direct documentary fact:** OSF consistently records 1979 as the launch of early scholarship philanthropy, and Soros himself described a 1980 visit and capacity of up to 80 recipients. **Inference:** the two may reflect program start versus a subsequent expansion/visit, but no contemporaneous records reviewed here settle that explanation. **UNVERIFIED:** actual first disbursement date, whether 80 individuals were paid in 1979, payment totals and primary UCT bursary ledger.
