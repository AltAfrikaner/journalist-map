## Claim examined: the foreign-correspondent denominator

The page used to note that the often-repeated **240-plus** correspondent figure was UNVERIFIED, but did not explain who can qualify for the association or why its membership cannot serve as a complete census of foreign reporting or Afrikaans proficiency.

- **Primary record:** [Foreign Correspondents' Association of Southern Africa, membership rules and rates](https://www.fcasa.org/contact-us). Full members are recognised resident correspondents for newsrooms headquartered outside Southern Africa. Freelancers must show qualifying contractual/full-time work over at least six months. **Associate** eligibility includes embassies, NGOs, UN and communications organisations; membership is therefore not synonymous with being a working foreign correspondent.
- **Separate official record:** [South African government's Foreign Media Directory listing of FCASA](https://www.gov.za/about-government/contact-directory/foreign-media/foreign-correspondents-association-southern-africa). This independently establishes the association's public contact listing, **not** individual membership numbers, language skills or a complete newsroom roster.
- **Research-map primary data:** The published HTML displays **28 journalists, 23 media houses, 61 connections**. These are the curated graph's displayed sample/edge counts, not an audited profession-wide census or proof of 61 causal editorial-control relationships. Individual edge provenance remains **UNVERIFIED**.

**Correction:** Expanded the visible FCASA text to distinguish member classes, residence and employer rules; removed any plausible impression that an association count or government contact directory proves the 240-plus figure. Explicitly interpreted 28/23/61 as **sample/map counts**, not externally verified headcounts. The concern about underrepresentation of Afrikaner sources remains a valid research question; no false measured zero or definitive exclusion finding is introduced.

**Red-team interpretation:** A foreign-language or international outlet may consult Afrikaans sources without an Afrikaans-speaking staff correspondent, and an Afrikaans-speaking journalist may not voluntarily disclose language competence. Full members, associates, freelance bylines, agencies and resident staff have different denominators. No evidence found in these two records can establish population-specific underrepresentation. A complete roster and publication-level source-diversity audit would be needed.

**Still UNVERIFIED:** any current full-member total, 240-plus claim, language/proficiency roster, all 28 individual employment timelines, evidence for each of the 61 drawn edges, named personal payments, and actual newsroom commissioning decisions.
