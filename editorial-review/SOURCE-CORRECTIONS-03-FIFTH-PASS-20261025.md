## Corrections actually made
1. **January decision versus April implementation.** The article's timeline now distinguishes Meta's **7 January 2025 US announcement** from its **7 April 2025 end of new US third-party fact-check labels**. Meta expressly described continued non-US programmes until separately implemented Community Notes launches. Nothing here establishes the status or outcome of a particular South African moderation case in October 2026. Corrected the date of the article's own editorial update.
2. **Claim-discovery mechanisms, not inferred commands.** Meta's primary **April 2024 South African election** statement says it used keyword detection to group related election content and was onboarding fact-checking partners to Meta Content Library. The article now explains these discovery tools do not by themselves show a predetermined Africa Check verdict, Afrikaans-specific targeting or a Meta penalty on a named post.
3. **Homepage contradicts reviewed article.** Removed the unqualified **OSF 12% / Gates 13%** current-funder presentation: Africa Check's public 2025 percentage graphic was not extracted into a verified year-matched denominator in this review. Changed the card's false **Africa Check → IFCN → Meta editorial relay** into the documented distinction: an accredited fact-checker publishes ratings, and Meta decides distribution under its own programme. This does not weaken legitimate scrutiny of selection bias or donor influence.

## Red-team alternatives and uncertainties
A fair critical reading is that government-election cooperation, programme-partner search tools and donor relationships create significant agenda-setting and appeal questions. A competing reading is that a platform supplies search infrastructure as normal part of fact checking and the NGO applies its own published methods; no donor instruction follows from the grant alone. Both readings require **individual examples** before alleging systematic anti-Afrikaner suppression. **UNVERIFIED:** original moderation notice, post-level rating and timeframe, demographic comparison group, any donor condition directing topic selection, current programme coverage in South Africa, 2025 audited donor-percentage breakdown.
