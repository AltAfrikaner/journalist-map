## Claim-level finding and on-page correction
The pre-existing dossier correctly documented Meta's April 2024 South African label/demotion mechanism but did not tell an affected publisher which organisation receives a disputed fact-check rating, how to preserve an appeal or why the timing matters. The new prose records Africa Check's own published appeal instructions, Meta's dated 30 October 2023 process and the Oversight Board's independent 26 March 2026 discussion. This is a substantive procedural evidence addition, not a finding that a particular Afrikaner post was penalised.

* **Fact-checker versus platform:** Africa Check directs a publisher to dispute the rating with the fact-checker or seek a changed rating after correcting the original. Deleting the original post can prevent appeal and does not erase a strike, under its published guidance.
* **Dated procedural limits:** Meta's authored 30 October 2023 statement, reproduced contemporaneously by Publicnow, describes an in-product appeal, seven days for a publisher to lodge a dispute/correction, and automatic rejection after fourteen days without processing. The user should not assume this 2023 guidance is an established 2026 South African service-level agreement.
* **Independent countercheck:** The Oversight Board's 26 March 2026 opinion describes third-party rating consequences and an appeal mechanism where the programme operates, but is not an audit of Africa Check appeal handling, South African notice dates, reversals or loss of reach.
* **UNVERIFIED:** Case-specific notification, exact October 2026 local appeal terms, individual appeals and dispositions, alleged ethnic/language targeting, and measurable recovery or sustained harm. Any accusation that Africa Check or Meta deliberately blocked a named Afrikaner case requires those records.
