## Correction 1 — original funding route, not an undated foundation coalition
**Before:** An imprecise sentence treated AFP Foundation support and the Wits hosting relationship as Africa Check's generic 2012 origin; the specific founding award was not distinguished from later foundation involvement.

**After:** The original initiative is dated to the **31 October 2012 Johannesburg launch**. The **IPI News Innovation Contest** award financed the seed stage, supported by Google, with the **AFP Foundation** developing the project and **Wits Journalism** as institutional partner. These are different legal and financial roles, and hosting alone does not establish control of fact-check verdicts.

**Independent evidence:** [AFP Foundation's 31 October 2012 press release](https://www.afp.com/en/agency/inside-afp/press-release/afp-foundation-launches-african-fact-checking-website-partnership) explicitly identifies the IPI award and original partners; [Nieman Journalism Lab's March 2014 reporting/interview](https://www.niemanlab.org/2014/03/the-nonprofit-africa-check-wants-to-build-more-fact-checking-into-the-continents-journalism/) independently attributes Google-backed IPI seed funds; [Africa Check's own funding history](https://africacheck.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-funded) calls IPI its sole launch funder. This is a description of an award channel, **not** proof of Google's involvement in later individual ratings.

## Correction 2 — 2015 West African funding belongs to the French-language expansion
**Before:** Later Open Society funding and the original 2012 launch could be read as a single undated financial network.

**After:** The 2015–16 timeline explicitly states that **OSIWA supported the Dakar-based French-language version launched in November 2015**. It does not retroactively classify OSIWA as the funder of the original Johannesburg launch, or as author of editorial conclusions.

**Primary records:** [AFP Foundation's 16 November 2015 French-language announcement](https://www.afp.com/fr/lagence/notre-actualite/communiques-de-presse/la-fondation-afp-annonce-le-lancement-dafrica-check) and [its contemporary English-language release](https://www.afp.com/sites/default/files/afpcommunique/201511/pdf/cpafricacheckfrancophonegb.pdf); compare the independent 2014 Nieman coverage of the earlier founding. **UNVERIFIED:** the exact OSIWA amount, recipient legal entity and spending restrictions without original award documents and accounts.

## Correction 3 — the Google/YouTube–IFCN grant was announced in 2022, not 2024
**Before:** The article said the Global Fact Check Fund disclosed Google/YouTube backing “in 2024,” conflating subsequent onward grants with the parent award.

**After:** The article records **29 November 2022**, **US$13.2m parent grant** and **US$12m Global Fact Check Fund**, distinguishing announcement, onward award and any Africa Check payment.

**Primary and independent recipient accounts:** [Google/YouTube 29 November 2022 announcement](https://blog.google/company-news/outreach-and-initiatives/google-news-initiative/how-google-and-youtube-are-investing-in-fact-checking/), [Poynter/IFCN recipient announcement](https://www.poynter.org/fact-checking/2022/google-and-youtube-partner-with-poynters-international-fact-checking-network-on-13-2-million-grant-for-the-global-fact-checking-community/). These sources do not specify an Africa Check share.
