## Source review (primary records and independent counterchecks)
1. [Meta, *More Speech and Fewer Mistakes*, 7 January 2025](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/01/meta-more-speech-fewer-mistakes/) (updated May 2025): Meta admits that labels and reduced distribution could over-enforce legitimate political speech, describes enforcement appeals, and says its phase-out began **in the United States**. Primary for Meta's announced policy, not proof it was followed in every South African account.
2. [IFCN/Poynter, *Signatory Benefits*](https://www.ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org/signatory-benefits): verification may be necessary but is **not sufficient** to obtain platform work; individual partnerships are direct and IFCN does not negotiate them. Primary for IFCN's own accreditation role; contrary to the source-page assertion of an IFCN-operated ratings API.
3. [PolitiFact, former Meta third-party partner, 7 January 2025](https://www.politifact.com/article/2025/jan/07/meta-ending-third-party-fact-checking-partnership/): first-hand description of case selection, near-duplicate matching, labels/downranking, and an appeal channel; independently organisational from Meta, but not disinterested in defending fact-checkers. Opposes an asserted **zero appeal** or mandatory account-poisoning process.
4. [TechCrunch, 16 February 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/16/yes-threads-is-running-a-fact-checking-program-but-the-system-isnt-fully-rolled-out/): independent reporting of Threads initially matching near-identical rated material before full direct fact-checking, contradicting a simple all-products-simultaneously theory.
5. [Oversight Board, 26 March 2026, *Country-Level Factors to Guide Community Notes Rollout*](https://www.oversightboard.com/news/board-provides-country-level-factors-to-guide-community-notes-rollout/): independent governance body warns Community Notes may introduce freedom-of-expression and human-rights risks in some countries. Counterweight to portraying withdrawal of professional fact-checking as universally beneficial. Does not establish South Africa's precise October 2026 rollout status.

## Red-team and right-of-reply treatment
**Best argument for original editorial thesis:** Meta itself acknowledged intrusive labels, reduced distribution and real mistakes in January 2025; professional fact-checkers disclose funders and are platform partners. These justify rigorous scrutiny of opaque moderation, conflicts of interest and impact on minority advocacy.

**Strongest rebuttal:** The reported donor shares mix years and denominators, IFCN neither owns every platform partnership nor directs all ratings, matching is not blanket political-topic suppression, and impact requires the original content, platform notice, dated applicable policy, matching fact-check and reach/appeal records. Neither named academic nor Africa Check has been shown directing a specific action against an Afrikaner farmer. The two diagrams are research models, not technical reverse-engineering evidence.

**Right of reply:** Public organisational positions are linked, but **no fresh case-specific response** was requested or obtained from Meta, Africa Check, IFCN, researchers or funders for this batch; nothing is presented as their private reply. A future adverse person-level allegation needs a documented opportunity to respond before release certification.

**Jurisdiction and dates:** Meta's 7 January 2025 announcement and 7 April 2025 US phase-out must not be read as October 2026 South African enforcement rules. The Oversight Board's 26 March 2026 advisory is country-conditional guidance, not proof of SA deployment. The 2024 Threads rollout documents historical product differences. The historic 3bn platform figure is not a measured audience exposed to one case.

**Numerical methodology:** 95% requires date, exposure denominator, geographic scope and either Meta disclosure or reproducible controlled reach data. The 3bn-affected count cannot be derived from MAU. Donation shares must state recipient, fiscal year and revenue denominator before aggregating. The historic zero/105 language-network totals require a defined universe and verified links.

**Remaining UNVERIFIED:** IDRC R4.5m/R20m award documentation, funder percentages and contract conditions; the named researcher's attributed farm-murder quotation and commissioning; Cunliffe-Jones's specified historical roles and any causal platform authority; OSF/Omidyar tool-grant terms; all South Africa-specific verified partner ratings, account consequences, reach estimates, and proposed cross-platform graph edges.
