## Exact changes, evidence and interpretations

1. **Stage 4 — an appeal existed, but not equally for every user.** Replaced generic appeals language with the [Oversight Board's 20 April 2023 primary interview record, paragraph 25](https://www.oversightboard.com/decision/pao-sabu4p2s/): Page managers and group admins could appeal some ratings in-product, but ordinary profiles lacked that path, rollout differed by country, and emailing the fact-checker was an alternative. The Board recommended a different reviewer for appeals (recommendation 6). **Do not mistake the 2023 description or recommendation for an October 2026 guarantee in South Africa.** The [Meta October 2023 appeal guidance](https://www.facebook.com/government-nonprofits/blog/fact-checking-on-facebook) and [former partner PolitiFact's 7 January 2025 account](https://www.politifact.com/article/2025/jan/07/meta-ending-third-party-fact-checking-partnership/) are additional institutionally interested provenance, not independent proof of a particular case. No seven-day appeal deadline was imposed on an unverified 2026 user.
2. **Stage 6 — the account-sanctions mechanism is real for repeat offenders, but the alleged targeted case is not demonstrated.** Previous wording risked implying that account sanctions from fact-check ratings were wholly hypothetical. [Meta's September 2026 signatory disclosure, Commitment 31](https://disinfocode.eu/reports/meta/9/text) says *repeated* False/Altered ratings can lead to recommendation, distribution, advertising and monetisation penalties in its reported **European** programme. The [Oversight Board's 2023 record, paragraph 29](https://www.oversightboard.com/decision/pao-sabu4p2s/) separately corroborates strikes and threshold penalties under the historical COVID-specific policy. The sources have different period and policy scope; neither supplies a universal current South African threshold. Rewrote Stage 6 without changing the hypothetical Afrikaner example into a documented case.

## Source independence and red team

- **Primary operating-policy source:** Meta's 2026 European Code of Conduct signatory report and its 2023 appeal guide. Meta's assertions are first-party disclosures and are not a third-party audit of practice.
- **Independent governance record:** Oversight Board policy advisory opinion, 20 April 2023, especially paragraphs 25, 29 and recommendations 6–7. The Board is separately constituted but financed by Meta; independence is qualified, and the subject was COVID-19 misinformation, not an Afrikaner case.
- **Independent newsroom/partner testimony:** PolitiFact's 7 January 2025 end-of-partnership report and [AFP's 10 September 2026 report](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/09/10/meta-tests-fact-check-replacement-in-latin-america) give historical and geographic context. Neither validates ethnic targeting.

**Affirmative finding:** The platform's own rules permit consequential sanctions against *repeat* rated content. **Contrary finding:** A specific account may have experienced a Community Standards action, ordinary ranking changes or a fact-check penalty; current evidence does not identify which. No institution-to-user command chain has been proven. **UNVERIFIED:** South African programme terms in October 2026, the original post, date, rating, account strike count, notice, appeal request and independent decision, grant conditions and selective-impact comparison.
