## Corrections made in the published narrative
1. **Review rights (stage 4):** Previously grouped rating, account-enforcement and Oversight Board appeals. Now identifies different decision-makers and refers to the 2023 Oversight Board recommendation to expand label appeals and avoid sending an appeal to the checker whose rating is challenged. Actual South African remedy **UNVERIFIED**.
2. **Review queue vs automated matching (stage 5):** Meta told the Oversight Board that the overwhelming majority of queued material was never externally reviewed. This rebuts a universal compulsory fact-check assumption; it does **not** quantify near-duplicate matching or 2026 South African routing. Claim-specific actions remain **UNVERIFIED**.
3. **Financial disclosure (body):** IFCN criterion 4.1 asks *independent* applicants to disclose funders accounting for at least 5% of *the preceding financial year's* revenue. This is not a global rule assigning percentages to all grantees or proof that a funding source controls ratings.
4. **Historic politician exception (body):** The Oversight Board's 2023 account and a former Meta partner agree that politicians/parties were not eligible for third-party checking. This does **not** mean their accounts were exempt from Community Standards or prove intent against minority communities. Comparable eligible-speaker sampling is necessary.
5. **Jurisdiction and 2026 chronology (body):** US label programme ended for new labels 7 April 2025. Meta started a **contributor-only** pilot in **16 Latin American countries on 9 September 2026**, retaining professional fact-checking in those markets during testing. The group does not include South Africa; specific SA October rules and case outcomes still **UNVERIFIED**.
