## Five visible corrections, not mere endnotes
1. **Stage 2 / queue and eligible speakers:** Meta told its Oversight Board in the April 2023 COVID-19 policy record that the vast majority of candidate posts in its fact-check queue were never reviewed; politicians and parties were historically exempt from the third-party programme. Those facts make automatic universal routing inaccurate and raise a structural equal-treatment question. Neither establishes an Africa Check-directed action against an Afrikaner post.
2. **Stage 4 / false universal notice:** Replaced a single alleged standard string `False Information. Checked by independent fact-checkers.` with policy-specific information: the Oversight Board reported obscuring interstitials for *False/Altered* and different treatment for *Partly False/Missing Context*. A dated screenshot is still required for the presentation on a particular South African case.
3. **Stage 5 / meaningful numbers and denominators:** Retained `up to 95%` solely as **UNVERIFIED** without a defined experiment. Added dated, narrowly scoped primary record: in a 90-day period preceding **9 December 2022**, Meta reported that approximately **10% of Facebook and 43% of Instagram users** exposed to a *False/Altered* warning uncovered its content. These are warning click-through proportions, **not** reach reductions, and have no Afrikaner numerator.
4. **Standards and grants:** IFCN criterion 4.1's >=5% previous-year revenue disclosure rule applies to an independent applicant; a fact-checking unit in a parent has different ownership-disclosure criteria. Funding percentages without same-year revenue ledgers, donor restrictions and recipient identity cannot show editorial direction.
5. **September 2026 pilot:** The Meta announcement of **9 September** covers 16 Spanish-speaking Latin American markets, initially invisible Community Notes and continued professional checking during the test. AFP confirms scope. It cannot certify South African programme status in October 2026.
