## Findings and corrections

1. **PIC chair wrongly described as the current Deputy Finance Minister.** South African Cabinet's [29 July 2026 meeting statement, published 30 July (primary government appointment register)](https://www.gov.za/news/cabinet-statements/full-statement-cabinet-meeting-29-july-2026-30-jul-2026) identifies **Seiso Joel Mohai**, Deputy Minister for Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation, as chair and lists seven other non-executive members, including a National Treasury representative. [Moneyweb, 30 July 2026 (independent reporting)](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/south-africa/seiso-mohai-appointed-new-pic-chair-as-cabinet-overhauls-board/) confirms he succeeded David Masondo. [Reuters, 23 July 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/south-africas-deputy-finance-minister-resigns-chair-state-asset-manager-2026-07-23/) dates Masondo's departure. Two reader-facing governance paragraphs now use the updated office title, rather than treating past leadership as incumbent.
2. **Cabinet appointment conflated with evidence of partisan membership/control.** Cabinet appointing PIC non-executive directors is documented. The old phrase *every board seat is a political appointment* could reasonably be read as proven ANC partisan allegiance of every director. That person-level claim, and any inference of direction of particular news coverage, remains **UNVERIFIED** without appointment, governance and instruction evidence. Changed the board paragraph, preserving scrutiny of pension stewardship.
3. **Dasnois dismissal timeline collapsed two events and asserted motive as settled.** The [Mail & Guardian's report dated 8 December 2013](https://mg.co.za/news/south-africa/2013-12-08-alide-dasnois-removed-as-cape-times-editor/) reports removal from the *Cape Times editorship* on **6 December 2013**. The [8 July 2014 report](https://mg.co.za/news/south-africa/2014-07-08-independent-newspapers-dismisses-former-cape-times-editor/) confirms later termination of employment, following disciplinary proceedings. The [Press Council's primary 1 August 2016 ruling](https://presscouncilsa.org.za/2016/08/01/alide-dasnois-vs-independent-newspapers/) discusses the settlement: editorial prerogative and withdrawal of racism allegations were recognised while the parties differed on the appropriateness of coverage. Corrected both the editorial-evidence card and historic timeline; did **not** rule conclusively on retaliation motives.
