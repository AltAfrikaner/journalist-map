## Claims tested and changed

1. **Misleading representation of a 2020 EU ethics opinion as an audited donor-ranking finding.** The previous “EU records: Sekunjalo was the most important donor” was overconfident. The [European Commission's 10 December 2020 Independent Ethical Committee opinion](https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2021-01/avis_cei_texte_10122020_en.pdf), paragraph 16, records *former Commissioner Dimitris Avramopoulos's explanation* that the Sekunjalo Development Foundation was Fight Impunity's “most important donor” at that time. It does not present a comprehensive donor audit, an official accounting determination or evidence of unlawful conduct by that donor. The first visible monetary-flow card now attributes this historical description to the witness.
2. **Payment confirmation versus motive and wrongdoing.** [Daily Maverick's 21 December 2022 reporting, including Iqbal Survé's response](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-12-21-iqbal-surves-sekunjalo-development-foundation-linked-to-organisation-at-centre-of-eu-corruption-scandal/) records his acknowledgement of a **R4.5 million** 2020 donation and denial of wrongdoing. Separately, [Italian reporting, 23 December 2022](https://www.corriere.it/politica/22_dicembre_23/qatargate-l-imprenditore-sudafrica-che-panzeri-ha-250-mila-euro-6f9d8460-8292-11ed-a908-044c2789a441.shtml) described financial support of about **€250,000**. Amounts and currencies come from different reporting records; do not sum them or treat them as an audited conversion without underlying transaction statements. The second Qatargate evidence card now includes the opposing account and clarifies: **a donation is not proof of bribery**; any donor-directed unlawful quid pro quo remains **UNVERIFIED**.

**Red team:** This correction does not erase the documented funding relationship or the public-interest question of whether it produced access or influence. Conversely, the EU opinion was considering former-Commissioner ethics, not conducting a criminal investigation or finding the South African donor culpable. Signed bank/NGO accounts, individual grant terms, invoices, related case judgments, corporate records, and contemporaneous beneficiary responses are required for a stronger causal claim.
