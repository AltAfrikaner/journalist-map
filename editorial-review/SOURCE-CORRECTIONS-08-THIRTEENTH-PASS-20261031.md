# Investigation #08 — USAID regional-asset audit: scope, exposure and questioned-cost precision (31 October 2026)

**OPEN — NOT RELEASE-CERTIFIED.** Additional primary-record cross-check focused on the risk of conflating a Pretoria-based *regional* portfolio with South African newsroom receipts, and audit risk with established misappropriation.

## Correction in `usaid.html`
- The **22 April 2026 USAID OIG report 4-674-26-001-P** (Introduction, p. 2, footnote 3) says the Pretoria-based *Regional Mission for Southern Africa* administered programmes in multiple countries and was **distinct from the bilateral USAID Mission in South Africa**. Its **51 terminated programmatic awards with high-value assets**, drawn from **87 terminated awards**, are **not** a count of South African newspaper payments.
- The OIG found **30 of 51** lacked approved disposition plans on **1 May 2025**, and sampled 16 awards with 614 high-value assets. The report does not claim all assets were stolen. The federal oversight register records **two open recommendations, $0 questioned costs, $0 funds for better use**. $0 questioned costs does not mean adequate controls or establish that nothing was ever lost; it means the audit did not assign financial questioned-cost findings.
- Reuters' **27 July 2026** independent reporting concerns the wider State Department transfer, staffing and systems shortfalls. It corroborates institutional-disruption context, **not** the 51-award numbers or proof of stolen South African newsroom funds.

## Adversarial test
**Blue team:** The report is direct evidence of weaknesses in USAID-funded asset accountability following programme cancellations. An investigation should seek asset registers, disposal approvals, recipient accounts and whether any assets were subsequently lost. **Red team:** A regional award is not a South African newsroom grant; an unapproved disposal plan is not a theft conviction. Exact diversion amounts, present asset whereabouts and downstream newsroom payments remain **UNVERIFIED**.
