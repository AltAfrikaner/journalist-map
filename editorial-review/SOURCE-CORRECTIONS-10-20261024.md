## Claim-level corrections

1. **Company officers versus shareholders.** The page headline had described the deaths of two prominent Iranian leaders on 28 February 2026 as the deaths of “both controlling shareholders”. Changed it to “Two Iranian Leaders Killed” and clarified that the legal corporate shareholder of the Irancell venture's 51% Iranian interest was the Iran Electronic Development Company, not the named individuals personally. Political leadership and corporate control should not be conflated. The resulting ownership, board appointments and operational control are **UNVERIFIED** without a current register.
2. **Subscriber figures.** The headline figure of 70 million was presented as a count of people under surveillance. This is not warranted by a telecom customer-count estimate. Revised to explicitly label surveillance as **UNVERIFIED**; seek dated original customer reports and corroboration before using the number as a definitive customer count.
3. **Court cases versus inquiry.** The label “5 Active US Lawsuits” was inaccurate: the five cards include an inquiry, a South African commercial case, an Irish dispute and US civil litigation. The hero label and legal section heading now identify the mixed set accurately. All five still require an independent current docket audit.
4. **Grand-jury status.** Removed a speculative extrapolation from the disclosed US inquiry to secret future events. A company-reported inquiry is neither a civil suit nor a verdict; the company said it had not been charged at disclosure. Further claims about the inquiry's undisclosed substance are **UNVERIFIED**.
5. **Accounting records.** Added an evidence box citing MTN's reviewed June 2026 interim statements and Reuters' 24 August 2026 report: the company's R3.9 billion joint-venture impairment is a valuation write-down, not an identifiable cash payment to a named entity. A financial relationship should be established through transaction and audited entity-level records.
6. **Judicial versus pleaded facts.** The box reiterates that South Africa's Supreme Court of Appeal decision of 29 April 2025 resolved jurisdictional/legal issues and was not a trial finding proving pleaded wrongdoing. It presents MTN's exit explanation as its claim rather than conclusive exoneration.
