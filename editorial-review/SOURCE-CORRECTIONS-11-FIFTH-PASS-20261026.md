## Editorial corrections made to `prototypenation.html`
1. **False vote attributed to South Africa (critical correction).** The 6 October 2022 UN Human Rights Council motion to discuss Xinjiang failed **17 in favour / 19 against / 11 abstentions**. **South Africa was not a member of the Council for that vote**; the General Assembly elected it on **11 October 2022** for the **2023–2025** term. The former page asserted a South African **NO** vote, which was impossible. Replaced with primary vote/session/election links and an independent AFP contemporary voting account. **Do not transpose voting records between the Human Rights Council and General Assembly.**
2. **Unbounded Israel roll-call assertion.** Replaced “voted FOR every UNHRC resolution without exception” with **UNVERIFIED** pending a complete dated roll-call analysis. The ICJ case was **instituted by South Africa** on **29 December 2023**; “co-sponsored” was inaccurate for institution of proceedings. Filing proves a lawsuit exists, not the truth of its accusations.
3. **Continuous Council membership.** Two glossary repetitions claiming uninterrupted South African Council membership since 2007 corrected: Council seats are elective and finite. October 2022 election was for January 2023–December 2025.
4. **Impossible ICC chronology.** The **Rome Statute was adopted 17 July 1998** and entered into force **1 July 2002**, establishing the International Criminal Court. The article/chart/glossaries' claim of ICC referrals “from 1994” is an anachronism. Commentary about an ideological international-law programme is distinguishable from documented court proceedings and remains **UNVERIFIED** absent specific records.
5. **Foreign-aid eligibility error.** South Africa's upper-middle-income World Bank grouping does **not** disqualify it from official development assistance: the OECD DAC's 2024–25 eligible-recipient list explicitly includes **South Africa** in that category. Whether particular assistance is effective, wasteful, politically motivated, or properly disbursed still warrants an award-level audit; categorically calling all aid “institutional maintenance” is unsupported.

## Red-team challenge
The article's comparison of differential international attention is a defensible subject for scrutiny, but a **false member-state vote must not be used as evidence of selective enforcement**. HRC resolutions, treaty-body concluding observations and UPR recommendations are different acts and cannot be combined into an undifferentiated “UN approval” denominator. ICC, ICJ and national policy debates similarly have distinct jurisdictions and histories. An OECD eligibility rule cannot establish whether a specific USAID project deserved funding. The article retains a critical editorial thesis; editorial conclusions are not documentary findings.
