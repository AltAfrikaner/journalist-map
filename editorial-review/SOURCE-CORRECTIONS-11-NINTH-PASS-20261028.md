## Direct evidence, disputed assertions and corrections

1. **UN mandate versus later monitoring:** General Assembly resolution **48/258A (23 June 1994)** terminated the dedicated Special Committee Against Apartheid on completion of its mandate. It did not terminate all UN scrutiny of South Africa. The lead paragraph now acknowledges the later treaty-body framework and its distinct legal powers, rather than implying UN-wide silence was a proven deliberate policy.
2. **2016/2023 CERD records:** The assertion that no UN body ever issued any criticism of post-1994 South Africa is untenable as a description of the record: UN experts addressed xenophobia, attacks on migrants and employment-equality measures in November 2023, and the Committee issued **CERD/C/ZAF/CO/9-11** concluding observations in December 2023. These are **not** UN Security Council sanctions or equivalent country-specific GA condemnation resolutions. Whether a targeted resolution was issued on each Afrikaner minority claim is **UNVERIFIED**; no exhaustive UN resolution index was compiled.
3. **Advocacy-law counts:** The on-page **116+** figure is left as the attributed AfriForum count; the card no longer states that the UN was silent on *every* instrument. Each listed law and CERD recommendation requires a dated item-by-item comparison. The number is not accepted as the UN's classification.
4. **Seven exports:** The intro previously called all seven policy/protest/funding/moderation pathways *documented South African exports*. This has been recast as a **comparative research map**: direct institutional transfers, policy analogies and alleged causal directions must be separately established. Countries with earlier equity laws cannot automatically be derivatives of 1994 reforms.
5. **Digital moderation export:** A visible export card's categorical South African origin/global command claim was recast as **UNVERIFIED**. Academic research, external fact-check ratings, accreditation and Meta enforcement are separate organisational decisions. The dated US-first January 2025 Meta change is a reminder not to assume uniform global policy.
