## Corrected publication claims
1. **Cross-national transitional justice timing:** The comparative table had said “Canada 2008, Australia, New Zealand. All cite SA TRC as model” with a blanket +13-year lag. New Zealand's Waitangi Tribunal was founded **1975**, well before the South African TRC legislation (**1995**). Australia's distinct Stolen Generations national inquiry began **1995**, with its report in **1997**; Canadian TRC followed in **2008**. These are not interchangeable institutional mechanisms, and a country-specific adoption channel must be demonstrated. Replaced the universal causal claim/lag with differentiated chronology and **UNVERIFIED** transfer. Primary: [Waitangi Tribunal](https://www.waitangitribunal.govt.nz/en/about/about-the-waitangi-tribunal/about-the-waitangi-tribunal), [Australia HRC](https://humanrights.gov.au/bringing-them-home/). Independent institutional account: [NZ government history](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/waitangi-tribunal-created). A documentary list of specific Canadian sources attributing institutional design to the South African model is not established here.
2. **Protest chronology:** The table claimed South Africa's Fees Must Fall “prefigures Western campus protests by 8 years” while invoking Black Lives Matter. BLM identifies its start as **2013**, confirmed independently by the [UK House of Lords Library](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/black-liberation-movements-then-and-now/). [South African History Online](https://sahistory.org.za/article/university-witwatersrand-student-protests-2015-timeline) records Fees Must Fall's launch in **October 2015**. That movement cannot have preceded a 2013 movement by eight years. It remains reasonable to compare anti-apartheid solidarity and later campus activism; particular operational influence and founders' alleged confirmation remain **UNVERIFIED**. The table's generic +20-year lag has no sound common starting/ending definition.
