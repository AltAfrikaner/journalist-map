## Corrections and primary-source check

1. **Wrong denominator in the Church Committee statistic.** The visible historical-inheritance timeline said, “nearly 50% of 700 international grants by US foundations were CIA-funded.” The **1976 Senate Church Committee Final Report, Book I, p. 182**, as reproduced on p. 50 of a [declassified CIA history held by the US National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/files/declassification/iscap/pdf/2014-090-doc-1-part-4.pdf), actually discussed **700 grants exceeding $10,000 from 164 foundations in 1963–1966, excluding Ford, Rockefeller and Carnegie; at least 108 received some CIA money**. The approximately half finding applied only to the *international-activities subset* of grants made by those smaller foundations. Therefore, the previous statement had both the wrong denominator and the wrong inclusion criteria. This materially matters: 108/700 is about **15.4%**, while the narrower international grant denominator was not stated there. We restored both the population and the limits.

2. **Ford/McCloy attribution and chronology.** The timeline previously made a definitive **1954** assertion about a Ford CIA liaison unit and a **$7m Ford-to-CCF** channel. [James Chace's independent historical assessment, *The New York Review of Books* (8 October 1992)](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1992/10/08/the-proconsul/) supports substantial McCloy/Ford cooperation with the CIA-backed Congress for Cultural Freedom, but the supplied material does not authenticate the precise 1954 unit-formation date or the claimed specific $7m transfer. Both are now **UNVERIFIED** pending contemporary Ford committee minutes, grant ledgers and bank records. The timeline label is now **1950s–1960s** instead of presenting the unproved year as a discrete establishment event.

3. **Unsupported 1987 IACF closure/end-of-funding claim.** The page previously said the Ford-financed successor continued until **1987**. The [University of Chicago Special Collections IACF finding aid](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.IACF) documents the successor association's historical archives through the late 1970s (including Ford correspondence dated 1977–1979). A separately published [historical chronology](https://legalclarity.org/congress-for-cultural-freedom-origins-cia-funding-and-collapse/) dates its formal dissolution to **1979**. We corrected the visible institutional end date, while distinguishing magazines that outlived the association. **UNVERIFIED:** the exact disbursement dates for each Ford subsidiary and any operational instruction or intelligence continuity after dissolution.
