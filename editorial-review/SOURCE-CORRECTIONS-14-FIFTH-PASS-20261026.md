## Claim-level changes

1. **Gini value and ranking — important cross-article correction.** The dashboard and evidence card claimed 'Gini 63.0' and 'world's most unequal' as undated current facts, conflicting with the site's corrected #11 inequality series. The [World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform / WDI](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SI.POV.GINI?locations=ZA-SE) gives South Africa's latest value as **54.1 in 2022** (0–100), with survey observations **59.3 (1993), 65.0 (2005), 59.6 (2014)**. The [Federal Reserve Bank of St Louis, FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/SIPOVGINIZAF) independently republishes the same World Bank data. This is *one underlying statistical series*, not two independent survey measurements. **Correction:** both on-page text metrics are dated, historical points and rankings explained. A current world 'worst' claim, without matched-year global comparisons, remains **UNVERIFIED**. Neither trend alone proves the psychological impact of a particular administration.
2. **Mental-health spending — historical survey mispresented as current.** The [original 2019 peer-reviewed national survey](https://academic.oup.com/heapol/article/34/9/706/5572608) measured **5.0% of the public health budget in FY2016/17**, with variation by province; the [UCT researchers' 2019 discussion](https://health.uct.ac.za/articles/2019-10-09-we-did-sums-south-africas-mental-health-spend-theyre-not-pretty) confirms this was historic system expenditure, not a 2026 budget. **Correction:** metric explicitly dated. The [South African Health Review workforce synthesis](https://sahr.hst.org.za/article/142351-south-africa-s-mental-health-human-resources-dilemma-from-shortage-to-solution) scopes its hospital gaps to **public hospitals offering mental-health services**, not every hospital. Its cited 2018 comparison estimates **0.27** national psychiatrists per 100,000, not the unsupported undated 0.28. The workforce source is secondary to an older study; no independent 2026 census was found.
3. **Child malnutrition — projection vs observation.** The visual dashboard presented the 2020 UNICEF **forecast** of 1.7m stunted children in 2025 as if observed. The page already cites [UNICEF's 2020 nutrition brief](https://www.unicef.org/southafrica/sites/unicef.org.southafrica/files/2020-07/ZAF-Nutrition-brief-2020.pdf) and [UNICEF's independent 2021 explanation](https://www.unicef.org/southafrica/press-releases/slow-violence-malnutrition-south-africa); both trace to UNICEF, so they are **not** two independent measurements. **Correction:** visual now explicitly says projected; the annual acute-malnutrition deaths and 20-year flat-rate claims flagged **UNVERIFIED** absent harmonised records.

## Red-team

A high level of inequality, mental-health underprovision and child malnutrition warrants critical scrutiny of government performance. It does not support undated global superlatives, diagnostic claims about every citizen, or a causal conclusion from disconnected surveys. The World Bank's 2022 Gini value does not show that inequality has disappeared; nor do 1993–2022 points alone demonstrate whether ANC policies increased it relative to counterfactuals. Hospital staffing and budget studies use different periods and denominators. These limitations are now disclosed on the page.
