## Claim-level evidence and correction

1. **A 2019 suicide figure presented prominently as 23.5 per 100,000 is no longer the figure displayed by the WHO's South Africa country profile.** WHO's official page reports **20.14 per 100,000 for 2019** with an **uncertainty interval of 15.96–24.11**. An older WHO-derived World Bank series, republished by IndexMundi, lists **23.5 for 2019**. Macrotrends independently republishes the newer **20.14** series, but neither republication is an independent mortality enumeration. This discrepancy is between releases of a WHO-modelled indicator, **not a demonstrated rise, fall or 2026 rate**. The displayed page KPI, dashboard value and source-wall evidence heading were aligned to the current WHO country-page value; the old 23.5 and historical **13,774 deaths** are retained explicitly as an **unreconciled earlier estimate**, not silently erased. Claims of national/global rank remain **UNVERIFIED** without common years and methods.
2. **Dommisse bibliography:** The paper's original complete title is *The Psychological Effects of Apartheid Psychoanalysis: Social, Moral and Political Influences*, not the abbreviated title formerly shown. Corrected the source bibliography and added a link to the original indexed record. This does not establish that a modern causal comparison is contained in the 1986 paper; the analogy remains a separate editorial interpretation.
3. This correction neither discounts the gravity of suicide or attributes the figure specifically to any one government policy. The older estimated death count and the revised rate cannot be treated as matched observed quantities without denominator and release documentation.
