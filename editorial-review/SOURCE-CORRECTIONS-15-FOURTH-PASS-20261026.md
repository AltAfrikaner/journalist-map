## Verified corrections
1. **Critical primary-text contradiction, 1992.** The existing chronology claimed that the word “nationalisation” had entirely disappeared from the ANC's post-Davos `Ready to Govern`. In fact the ANC's May 1992 adopted economic policy explicitly listed **nationalisation** among optional ways to increase the public sector, alongside equity purchases, new state corporations and public-private joint ventures. It also embraced a dynamic private sector. This is not evidence of a final privatisation command issued at Davos. An independent published historian traces actual 1992 internal ANC debate, contrary to the original absolute conference-denial claim.
2. **2019 minister and institutional chronology.** South Africa's 16–17 April 2019 WEF affiliate-centre agreement was announced by **Science and Technology Minister Mmamoloko Kubayi-Ngubane**, not Blade Nzimande. Source is the signing ministry. The 2021 CSIR account says it assumed host duties and was affiliated to the WEF; independently reported June 2021 CSIR/Deloitte MoU records operational support. University of Pretoria's September 2025 SACIT launch calls its new centre the first South African member of the 22-centre WEF C4IR network. **Conflict recorded, not resolved by choosing one institution's press release:** historic affiliation tier, changed network rules, and legal/contractual continuity **UNVERIFIED** without original 2019, 2021 and 2025 agreements.
3. **Governance and budget.** A documented WEF-affiliated policy forum does not itself grant WEF lawmaking/veto authority; public spending and policies must be traced to actual budgets, workplans and South African departmental decisions, not assumed from agency affiliation.
4. **Date and future:** Ramaphosa 1993 to presidency in 2018 is **25**, not 26, years. Continuous mentoring / instruction over that period is **UNVERIFIED**. The announced 2027 WEF event has not occurred as of this review; prior “arc complete” language wrongly implied it had.
