# Investigation #19 — refugee policy/admission chronology (fourth pass)

**Reviewed:** 25 October 2026. **Page:** `44afrikaners.html` (homepage #19). **Status: OPEN, not evidence-certified.**

## Evidence and contrary-source review

1. **Primary U.S. record:** [Executive Order 14204, signed 7 February and published 12 February 2025 in the Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/02/12/2025-02630/addressing-egregious-actions-of-the-republic-of-south-africa). It directed officials to *prioritise* humanitarian relief, admission and resettlement for qualifying Afrikaners. It did **not** grant automatic refugee status to all white South Africans. The order records the U.S. administration's allegations of unjust racial discrimination; it is evidence of the administration's policy, not independent adjudication of every factual allegation.
2. **Independent arrival and legal-process reporting:** [Reuters, 12 May 2025](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/first-white-south-africans-fly-to-us-under-trump-refugee-plan-4038110), reported **59** white South Africans welcomed with refugee status in the first widely reported arrival group. The article attributes persecution concerns to the U.S. government and describes the South African government's rejection of that characterisation. [Reuters, 7 February](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-signs-executive-order-aimed-south-africa-white-house-official-says-2025-02-07/) independently reported the earlier executive-order *plan*, before specific arrivals.
3. **Relevant on-page contrary arguments:** The open letter, AfriForum response and participant denials already recorded in [the third-pass evidence register](SOURCE-CORRECTIONS-19-THIRD-PASS-20261025.md) remain distinct; the existence of a US refugee route does not establish either the letter's representative mandate or government coordination behind it.
