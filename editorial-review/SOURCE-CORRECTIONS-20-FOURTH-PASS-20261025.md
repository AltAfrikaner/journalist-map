## Primary records and independently reported procedure

1. **Primary SIU evidence:** [SIU interim report statement, 29 September 2025](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/special-investigating-unit-releases-interim-report-tembisa-hospital) reports **1,728 purchase-order bundles worth R816,560,710** under review in the Maumela grouping, with **three suppliers associated with Vusimuzi Matlala, awarded a total of R13,538,292** at Tembisa. This is a documented *procurement overlap* between named persons in the separate Tembisa matter; it is not evidence that the 2021 assassination caused or financed the SAPS tender. The SIU's 207 current service providers under investigation should not be silently equated to Deokaran's earlier flagged 217 entities.
2. **Primary civil outcome:** [Ministry of Justice, 20 July 2026](https://www.justice.gov.za/m_statements/2026/20260720-ForfeitureOrder-Maumela-Min.html) announced a court forfeiture order for approximately **R326 million** of assets linked to Maumela's case. Civil property proceedings are distinct from criminal convictions of all reported associates. These assets and Tembisa orders must not be added to the SAPS Medicare24 tender headline.
3. **Independent March arrest chronology:** [TimesLIVE, 24 March 2026](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-03-24-twelve-top-cops-arrested-for-involvement-in-cat-matlalas-r360m-contract/) reports twelve senior SAPS officers **arrested on 24 March**, with court appearances on 25 March. This fixes the article's one-day date ambiguity; arrests are allegations, not judgments.
4. **Independent procedural counterevidence:** [ECR, 13 July 2026](https://www.ecr.co.za/news/news/matlala-withdraws-from-plea-deal-agreement/) reports Matlala withdrew the plea-and-sentence agreement after the proposed eight-year effective term was rejected; the agreement was declared null. Neither a final conviction nor an ongoing guilty plea can be inferred from the abandoned bargain.
5. **Primary prosecution decision and executive follow-through:** [NPA, 15 September 2026](https://www.npa.gov.za/index.php/media/npa-withdraws-charges-against-national-commissioner-south-african-police-service-general) independently reviewed and ordered withdrawal of PFMA charges against Commissioner **Fannie Masemola**, citing lack of reasonable prospects of success; [the Presidency, 16 October 2026](https://www.presidency.gov.za/president-ramaphosa-uplifts-suspension-national-commissioner-south-african-police-service) confirms court withdrawal **22 September** and his return **19 October**. Independent [SABC, 22 September](https://www.sabcnews.com/sabcnews/court-withdraws-all-charges-against-masemola-in-medicare24-tender/) separately reported the same court disposition. That withdrawal does not decide the other defendants' cases.

## Corrected article finding

The original visible finding collapsed three materially different cases into an apparently proved chain: whistleblower Deokaran's 2021 murder, SIU's Tembisa Hospital procurement allegations, and SAPS's 2024 Medicare24 award. It ended categorically "flag, kill, protect, reward, unravel", implied the President's relation was part of a single proven 217-company scheme, and dated SAPS arrests a day late.

The revised human-readable paragraphs cite SIU's actual **R13.54m three-supplier intersection** (a stronger, narrower lead than mere circumstantial names), distinguish **R816.56m under review** from proven loss or money paid, state the **R326m civil asset forfeiture** separately, and bring subsequent Matlala plea withdrawal and Masemola charge withdrawal/return into the record. Presidential involvement, Mbalula involvement in the alleged Tembisa scheme, a murder-to-tender directing chain and guilty findings beyond the actual orders are **UNVERIFIED**.

**Red-team:** These original findings merit careful investigation, but the SIU's concrete overlapping suppliers do not establish coordination of a murder or intervention by the President or ANC secretary-general. Separately, the NPA's dropping Masemola's prosecution does not prove that procurement at SAPS was lawful. Both hypotheses must be tested against the correct individual, case and document.
