## Evidence-led corrections to prominent published claims

1. **H.R. 2633 identity and chronology (primary legislation vs inference).** The 119th Congress's H.R. 2633 is the **U.S.-South Africa Bilateral Relations Review Act of 2025**, introduced **3 April 2025** — *not* the “Global Corruption Accountability Act.” The latest published legislative action reviewed on **29 October 2026** is the **22 July 2025** House Foreign Affairs Committee order to report an amended bill, **not enactment or final House passage**. Under **Section 6 of the introduced text**, a *classified report* identifying potentially sanctionable officials would be due **120 days after enactment**. The report is a proposed future requirement, not proof of an existing classified list, that Mbalula is named, or that the US Treasury has already made a designation. The former “34–16” committee vote was not preserved because the published tracker says **34–13** and a vote total is unnecessary to this chronology.
2. **Magnitsky sanctions petition vs government action (primary advocacy and independent coverage).** AfriForum's **24–25 March 2026** dossier publicly requested US consideration of Global Magnitsky sanctions. This is an advocate's request, **not** an OFAC confirmation of a pre-designation review, a Treasury investigation naming Mbalula, or an actual sanctioned-person listing. Claims about an undisclosed live OFAC case and a Tembisa-related US thread remain **UNVERIFIED**.
3. **Leaked Russian-agent allegations vs confirmed facts.** Forbidden Stories and journalistic partners published leaked-memorandum allegations concerning a **23 December 2024** meeting and a purported **US$3,000 monthly payment** to Mbalula adviser Bongani Mbindwane. Both Mbalula and Mbindwane disputed key claims. A reported intelligence memo is evidence of a *reported allegation*, not independently authenticated hotel attendance or bank transfers. The article now identifies the allegation, reporting provenance, denials and **UNVERIFIED** gaps.
4. **The so-called six-part protective architecture is a hypothesis.** The earlier prose described six mechanisms as an operationally proven shield, with categorical claims about a relative in the NPA Asset Forfeiture Unit, dictated media coverage and foreign-state mutual deterrence. These were replaced with source-sensitive cautions: the AFU principally seeks civil recovery and forfeiture rather than prosecuting every underlying crime; potential family conflict needs appointment, case-allocation and recusal records; historic share percentages require dated corporate filings and do not establish editorial instructions; leaked allegations do not prove an intelligence relationship.
5. **Distinguish findings and legal decisions.** The **December 2018 Public Protector executive-ethics finding**, the **August 2023 NPA decision not to prosecute** in the Dubai-holiday matter and **AfriForum's 2026 challenge** have different thresholds. None alone establishes a six-part obstruction conspiracy. Likewise an ANC official's political influence is not proof of interference.
6. **Five purported “independent converging mechanisms.”** The previous section conflated a commission of inquiry, journalism about leaks, a proposed private prosecution, an unenacted congressional bill and an alleged secret OFAC review, calling the combination “first in SA history.” It now identifies **five distinct research leads**, disclaims a verified historical first, and distinguishes accusations from institutional/legal outcomes. The research-board introduction likewise says lines and strings are hypotheses rather than invariably certified edges.
