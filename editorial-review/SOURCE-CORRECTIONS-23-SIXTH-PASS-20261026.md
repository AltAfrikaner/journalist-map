## Source-led corrections, including contrary evidence

1. **IBM — the missing recipient distinction.** The article's R700m technology figure derives from IBM South Africa's **2015 ten-year equity-equivalent B-BBEE investment programme**, not a disclosed unrestricted R700m grant to Wits. The South African Department of Trade and Industry described the enterprise/academic/research programme and equity-equivalent ownership credits. Engineering News reported the Wits infrastructure was provided **rent-free** for the laboratory; this does not establish an audited full R700m receipt by Wits. Separate **June 2019 IBM Q** cloud-access programme included Wits plus 15 other universities, not the same investment or proof all participants received the R700m. The 2015 university annual report's phrasing is broad and conflicts with an assumption of university cash: reconcile programme expenditures with Wits's accounts, contracts and independently audited beneficiary schedule. Corrected finance card, summary and research-outcome language, **without editing SVG**.
2. **Gates — commitments versus cash.** DevelopmentAid's **1 April 2026** analysis reports **47 grant approvals / approximately US$80m** in the 2024–25 tally to **Wits Health Consortium**. Individual Gates Foundation grant records name the Consortium as grantee, define multi-month purposes and commit amounts. Neither source proves payments were all made by 2025, fungible for university governance, nor that criticism of university policy endangers medical research. Replaced the unfounded 'institutional untouchability' description with a funding-governance question.
3. **Citation and command claims.** Reclassified repeated **81,780 Mbembe citations**, universal curricula assertions and a Gaspard-to-Wits instruction-chain insinuation as **UNVERIFIED** where text implied settled fact. A dated Scopus/Google Scholar/Web of Science export, syllabus sample, individual funder agreement and communications are required. University academic relations, ambassadorial service and OSF leadership may be real while directed editorial control remains unproven.
