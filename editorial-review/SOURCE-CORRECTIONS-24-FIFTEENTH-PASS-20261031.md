## Three distinct stages in the Volkstaat Council's closure

**Before:** the comparison table compressed closure into “Defunded 1999; repealed 2001”; the timeline claimed vaguely that the mechanism was “eliminated” in 2001 and attributed a chairman quotation without a dated primary recording. That was temporally imprecise and could wrongly imply the repeal eliminated the **entire constitutional principle** of cultural self-determination.

**After:** the article separates (1) the 1994 statutory enabling mandate, (2) independent contemporary reporting on 1 April 1999 that the government body had ceased and would continue privately, and (3) **Act 30 of 2001** (assented to 12 November, gazetted 23 November) that repealed **s184A and selected s184B(1) functions, deemed effective 30 April 2001**. The 1996 Constitution still contains section 235, which acknowledges a possible community self-determination arrangement subject to national legislation. A retained text is not equivalent to a dedicated council or implemented territorial autonomy.

- Original 1993 interim constitution as amended in 1994: [Government text — s184A–B](https://www.gov.za/documents/constitution/constitution-republic-south-africa-act-200-1993-repealed-28-jan-1994).
- Primary 2001 statute: [Repeal of Volkstaat Council Provisions Act 30 of 2001, government](https://www.gov.za/tn/documents/acts/repeal-volkstaat-council-provisions-act-30-2001-23-nov-2001), especially commencement clause; independent statute publication [LawLibrary](https://lawlibrary.org.za/akn/za/act/2001/30/eng%402001-11-23).
- Independent contemporary: [*Mail & Guardian*, 1 April 1999](https://mg.co.za/news/south-africa/1999-04-01-volkstaat-council-goes-private/), quoting SABC announcement of organisational disbandment; this does not, by itself, prove a 1999 **legal repeal**.
- 1996 primary constitutional provision: [s235 in final Constitution](https://www.gov.za/documents/constitution/constitution-republic-south-africa-1996-chapter-14-general-provisions-07-feb).

**Documented:** dedicated institutional mechanism lost legal powers, but abolition of the council cannot be conflated with repeal of s235. **Inference:** practical protections were less enforceable after the dedicated council's closure. **UNVERIFIED:** successor institutions' practical equivalence, funding details and whether all 1994 self-determination negotiation obligations were legally extinguished.
