## Claim corrections, contrary evidence and provenance

1. **Unspecified -80% GDP-growth collapse removed from prominent numeric card, NOT quietly retained as an established metric.** Statistics South Africa's annual Q4 2025 GDP release (10 March 2026) gives **1.1% real output growth for calendar 2025** and **0.5% in 2024**. Independent Reuters (10 March 2026) independently reported the 1.1% outcome and described its weakness against forecasts. The card now displays the dated **1.1%** growth rate with a clear statement that the original -80% alleged decline since 1994 is **UNVERIFIED**. A contemporaneous growth rate is not GDP's real level and not a 32-year causal comparison; a historical collapse percentage needs matched start/end years, rebasing/revisions, indicator definition and calculation.
2. **Demographic constitutional scorecard attribution corrected.** Prominent body copy had claimed +6 population/-3 Coloured constitutional effects were measured provision-level gains/losses, although there was no coding schedule. This is a historical hypothesis, not a replicated metric. Adjacent text now labels **all plotted scores UNVERIFIED**, acknowledges that equality, property, education-language and community-rights provisions require jurisprudential reading, and notes that a provision's existence does not measure access to public services. We retained original infographic values unchanged so readers can see the contested original claims.
3. **Counterinterpretation:** South Africa has experienced weak long-run growth; the 1.1% headline does **not** prove success, remedy unemployment, or establish constitutional causation. Conversely, low growth does not establish an -80% collapse relative to an unspecified 1994 baseline. Signed constitutional text and certification are verifiable but do not settle the distributive effects among Afrikaans-speaking, Coloured, black or other communities. The concern about degraded institutional language access remains a defensible analytical question.
