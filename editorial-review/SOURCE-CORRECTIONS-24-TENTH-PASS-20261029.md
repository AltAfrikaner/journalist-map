This conflicts with the final Constitution's qualified rights and court-record evidence that Afrikaans tuition continued, though reduced. The summary treated shorter language clauses as lost rights and longer property clauses as additional state power, without a legal coding framework.
- **Revised wording:** preserves the original six graphic figures and contestable political criticism, but labels the zero claim **UNVERIFIED**. A provision's replacement or shortening is not numerically equivalent to elimination of all legal protection.
- **Primary judicial record:** [Constitutional Court, *Gelyke Kanse v Stellenbosch University*, 10 October 2019](https://www.concourt.org.za/index.php/judgement/338-gelyke-kanse-and-others-v-chairperson-of-the-senate-of-the-university-of-stellenbosch-and-others-cct311-17) upheld the 2016 language policy; this qualified outcome was not a declaration that Afrikaans study disappeared. The concurring judges also raised concern over systemic English dominance.

It does not establish a 2026 census of all universities.
