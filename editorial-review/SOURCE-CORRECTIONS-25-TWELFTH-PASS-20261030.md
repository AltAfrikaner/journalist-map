## New evidence and editorial correction

1. **Published foundation criticism after leadership turnover.** On 21 October 2026 the FW de Klerk Foundation publicly objected that a sworn-statement condition for municipal-election objections appeared in final electoral regulations but not the consultation draft. This first-party statement is dated and has an identifiable claim about process, not evidence of general donor instructions.
2. **Authoritative amendment cross-check.** Electoral Commission **Notice 4215 of 2026, Government Gazette 55621, 23 October 2026** replaced the regulation. The published text of 28F allows written section 65 objections by the specified email route before 17:00 on the second day after voting **without the extra oath condition**. This is a primary legal document, not an inferred political act.
3. **Independent and adversarial checks.** Mzansi Political Safari, 25 October, independently reported the narrow voting/counting-objection route and explicitly distinguished it from every other electoral complaint. The Foundation's 27 October follow-up welcomed the correction. This **contradicts a categorical narrative that its constitutional watchdog work disappeared** after the director's September departure. Neither the timing nor the Foundation's self-description independently establishes that its intervention caused the IEC change, that other lines of scrutiny were maintained, or that external funders directed the organisation. These latter propositions remain **UNVERIFIED**.
4. **Chronological transparency.** The original hero's *May 2026* article date lacked warning that the body includes September–October 2026 developments; the date line now distinguishes original label from 30 October evidence-review date.
