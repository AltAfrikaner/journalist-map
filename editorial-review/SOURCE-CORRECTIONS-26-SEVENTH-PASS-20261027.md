## Distinct documented sources

1. **Named recipient/project/year (primary grant register):** [The Other Foundation's grants allocated](https://theotherfoundation.org/grants-allocated/) specifically lists **Church World Service — advocacy training on working with church leaders — South Africa — ZAR100,000** in the **2019** allocation section. This is stronger recipient-level evidence than generic network adjacency, but it proves the foundation's **published allocation**, not independently audited bank transfer or how any government donor earmarked the award. The Other Foundation also lists [trustee review and grant decision arrangements](https://theotherfoundation.org/about-us/), which must not be silently treated as a direct US State Department instruction.
2. **Separate US refugee-processing programme (primary US government report):** The [State Department's FY2025 refugee-admissions report to Congress](https://2021-2025.state.gov/report-to-congress-on-proposed-refugee-admissions-for-fiscal-year-2025/) identifies a FY2024 PRM cooperative agreement with CWS to run RSC Africa in Nairobi. PRM funding for prescreening support is not a DRL/Global Equality Fund grant or USCIS refugee eligibility decision.
3. **Independent operational check:** [Reuters, 17 December 2025](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/south-africa-arrests-kenyans-working-us-refugee-applications-2025-12-17/) independently reports the South African immigration arrests and names CWS's RSC Africa role. Reuters' reporting does not show that any particular grant induced litigation or discriminatory applicant handling.
