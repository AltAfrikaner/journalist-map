## Correction and evidence
The visible Welcome Corps entity card called CWS and IRAP “joint implementers,” implying a two-organisation operation, and tied both to State Department programme delivery and Democracy Fund financing while elsewhere describing *Pacito* as amended in April 2026. The [US State Department's 19 January 2023 fact sheet](https://2021-2025.state.gov/launch-of-the-welcome-corps-private-sponsorship-of-refugees-2/) identifies a **Community Sponsorship Hub-led consortium** with CWS, IRIS, IRAP, IRC and Welcome.US. The [Community Sponsorship Hub's contemporaneous launch statement](https://communitysponsorshiphub.org/news-stories/csh-welcome-corps/) independently describes the consortium; it was not a bilateral CWS/IRAP command arrangement.

More importantly, [IRAP's own dated launch statement](https://refugeerights.org/news-resources/irap-celebrates-launch-of-private-sponsorship-program-for-refugees) expressly calls IRAP an **unfunded consortium partner** responsible for legal resources. [CWS's own PSO page](https://cwsglobal.org/take-action/community-involvement/neighbor-network/welcome-corps/) describes a separate private-sponsor technical-assistance role. “Federal funding of a consortium” is not evidence that IRAP itself received a programme grant. A dated court chronology already audited on the previous pass establishes the **16 June 2026 operative *Pacito* amended complaint**, not April filing. No record here proves that Democracy Fund's separate grants financed Welcome Corps, *Pacito*, or instructions to refuse Afrikaner admissions; those inferences are **UNVERIFIED**. The overlap remains a legitimate institutional accountability question.
