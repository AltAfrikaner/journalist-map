## Correction and evidence threshold

1. **False universal equivalence removed.** Section 04 previously declared that private philanthropy and USAID do "the same work in the same countries with the same grantees," then inferred that the whole private architecture had outlasted public aid. Documented co-funding in one programme is not proof of universal matching or a joint decision chain. We corrected the prominent summary to specific overlap and distinguished parallel private capital from public appropriations.
2. **178 grants are not 178 South Africa grants.** The [Making All Voices Count original archive](https://www.makingallvoicescount.org/) states June 2013–November 2017, 178 programme-wide awards: **72 innovation + 38 scaling + 7 tech-hub + 61 research = 178**. The [programme's 2015 research strategy](https://www.makingallvoicescount.org/publication/making-all-voices-counts-research-and-evidence-strategy/) names **six priority countries**: Ghana, Indonesia, Kenya, Philippines, South Africa and Tanzania. The [2018 programme learning review](https://www.makingallvoicescount.org/publication/supporting-innovation-use-technologies-accountability-initiatives-lessons-making-voices-count/) and [independent OGRX overview](https://ogrx.org/organizations/making-all-voices-count.html) confirm the implementation consortium **Hivos / IDS / Ushahidi** and donors **DFID / Sida / USAID / Omidyar Network**. Lower-intensity countries included Bangladesh, Liberia, Mozambique, Nigeria, Pakistan and Uganda. The page now distinguishes priority geography from the additional lower-intensity reach, rather than presenting a 12-country sample as identical effort in every country.
3. **Corrected the visible MAVC case card and tag**, not only an endnote: the role, description and grant-geography label now give the source-supported split. The legacy SVG/3D research diagram is **not independently claim-certified**, and its broad geography/causal labels must be treated as **UNVERIFIED** until graph-edge verification. No South African recipient-by-recipient award total or private-donor instructions were established by this pass.

## Red-team objection and evidence still missing

The most favourable alternative reading is that governments and philanthropy funded a defined civic-technology programme with separate independent implementers, not that funders were a single operational command. The counterargument worthy of further scrutiny concerns actual contractual governance, partner voting rights, recipient selection, project-level disbursements, editorial obligations and demonstrable measurable effects; underlying contracts and South African grant registers remain **UNVERIFIED**.
