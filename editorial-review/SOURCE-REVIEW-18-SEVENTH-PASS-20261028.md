## Evidence and conflicts

1. **Reported 2004 proposed Rooivalk transaction versus proved export.** June 2012 reporting recounts former MTN Irancell director Chris Kilowan's testimony that South African and Iranian figures discussed weapons (Rooivalk attack helicopters, G5 artillery, radar) in **2004**. The former minister Mosiuoa Lekota rejected his account, described it as hearsay and denied meeting Kilowan. No authenticated Rooivalk contract, equipment export licence, end-user certificate or shipment manifest was established by this audit; an alleged meeting is not a completed sale.
2. **2009 Bell 212 route distinct from Rooivalk.** Sunday Times reporting (11 March 2012) alleged a route for **Bell 212** helicopters via South African front entities to Iran in **2009**. On **15 March 2012**, NCACC inspector Vanessa du Toit confirmed an **investigation**, not a definitive finding. These allegations do not establish a Rooivalk shipment, MTN's role in a particular flight, or any adjudicated sanctions breach. Ownership, end users, classification, dates and finances **UNVERIFIED**.
3. **UN law chronology:** UNSC resolution **1929 dated 9 June 2010** restricted sale/transfer of specified heavy weapons, including *attack helicopters*, **to Iran**. A 2010 restriction cannot alone criminalise a claimed **2009** shipment; domestic licensing law, earlier sanctions, equipment classification and actual date require independent verification.
4. **Turkcell's procedural appeal is not a bribery verdict.** The **29 April 2025 SCA judgment [2025] ZASCA 50** sets out disputed pleadings and legal jurisdiction. It does not find liability on alleged Iranian licence bribery or prove any arms delivery. September 2026 Constitutional Court hearing reporting indicated judgment reserved. No authenticated merits judgment or awarded damages identified in this review.
