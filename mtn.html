Background

Who Is MTN Group?

🇿🇦 MTN Group Limited is Africa's largest mobile telecommunications company, headquartered in Johannesburg, South Africa, and listed on the JSE (Johannesburg Stock Exchange). Founded in 1994, MTN reported 317.7 million subscribers at 30 June 2026 in its interim results. Earlier, Reuters' November 2025 report counted 301 million customers across 16 African markets. MTN's Iran interest is separately reported as an equity-accounted 49% joint venture in its 2026 financial reporting, not as one of those 16 African markets. The October 2025 300-million milestone was historical, not the latest count. An assertion that MTN ranked sixth among all global operators remains UNVERIFIED without a dated ranking and consistent subscriber denominator.

In 2005, MTN obtained a licence to operate a mobile network in 🇮🇷 Iran through a venture called MTN-Irancell. The licence was originally awarded to Turkish rival Turkcell, but MTN allegedly used bribes, promises of South African military weapons, and diplomatic pressure at the IAEA (International Atomic Energy Agency) to replace Turkcell and secure the contract. Turkcell’s lawsuit and former MTN executive Chris Kilowan described an alleged MTN plan code-named “Project Snooker”; the phrase appears in reporting about disputed memos, not a court finding of the alleged scheme. Reuters reporting republished by News24 (18 June 2012) describes Kilowan’s testimony and MTN’s rebuttal. Mail & Guardian (29 March 2012) independently reports the disputed alleged memos. Their authenticity, effect and alleged corrupt purpose require adjudicated evidence.

MTN's Irancell venture involves Iranian shareholders holding 51% and MTN's group holding a 49% interest. The Iranian shareholder entities and alleged links to military or religious-state structures warrant due diligence. Their legal shareholding, ultimate beneficial ownership, operational access to subscriber information and alleged influence on military intelligence are different facts that require separately dated evidence.

Could Subscriber Data Reach Missile Targeting?

Iranian strikes against sites in the Gulf, including the US Fifth Fleet presence in Bahrain, are part of the February 2026 conflict record. What the courts have ruled, and what remains alleged

MTN's Iranian licence is a legitimate subject of scrutiny. In South Africa the Turkcell/East Asian Consortium litigation concerns allegations that MTN used improper diplomatic, commercial and arms-related promises to secure its position in Irancell. The Supreme Court of Appeal's April 2025 judgment records the allegations and competing defences but dealt with jurisdiction and legal issues, not a trial verdict proving the original bribery or weapons promises. A complaint, affidavit, corporate licence and final adjudication do not all have the same evidential status.

The September 2026 US ruling is procedural, not a guilty verdict

On 25 September 2026, the United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York denied MTN's motion to reconsider earlier rulings allowing specified claims to proceed in Zobay v MTN Group Limited (No. 21-cv-3503). In a separate order it declined to certify an immediate interlocutory appeal. The published court order explains that the prior motions concerned whether the pleadings plausibly alleged knowing and substantial assistance under the Anti-Terrorism Act. At this stage the court was not weighing all trial evidence and did not find that MTN had committed the alleged acts.

MTN's 29 September 2026 shareholder statement independently confirms the procedural outcome and describes the pending Zobay and Long cases. The company disputes wrongdoing and has previously stated that the plaintiffs sued the wrong parties; it argues that it will have opportunities to test the facts through discovery, later motions or trial. Readers should treat that explanation as MTN's position, not as a judicial rejection of the claims. The published litigation position therefore establishes an ongoing contested case. Criminal guilt or civil liability is UNVERIFIED.

SS7 vulnerabilities are not evidence of missile-targeting use

Telecommunication signalling, roaming and shared network infrastructure can raise genuine national-security questions. The key distinction is between a capability that exists in a protocol, a connection that exists between carriers, and proof that a particular actor used the connection for a named military operation. For the latter, investigators would need verified relevant carrier interconnection records, a credible attribution of system access, technical logs or forensic reporting, and independent evidence tying data to a missile attack. This article does not provide that case-level record. No legitimate inference from SS7's vulnerabilities alone can identify a missile's guidance data source.

Similarly, MTN's minority ownership in a telecommunications operation with Iranian entities does not itself prove that MTN executives instructed surveillance, approved illegal arms transfers or participated in foreign attacks. Conversely, the existence of company denials does not dispose of the underlying allegations. A credible examination should compare the Turkcell civil pleadings, the South African court's jurisdictional ruling, the US court's procedural orders, shareholder filings and independently verified operational evidence. If an extraordinary technical claim cannot be sourced to that standard, mark it as UNVERIFIED.

Editorial conclusion: Historical contractual and shareholding relationships, allegations of political inducements, a public civil lawsuit, and the court's decision to let some claims proceed are distinguishable and important facts. A demonstrated chain from MTN-Irancell subscriber data into the February 2026 missile guidance system has not been established here. Primary court record: what the 2025 ruling decided

In East Asian Consortium B.V. v MTN Group Limited [2025] ZASCA 50 (29 April 2025), South Africa's Supreme Court of Appeal addressed jurisdiction and related legal defences in litigation involving alleged impropriety in the Iranian GSM licence. The appeal ruling was not a trial finding that alleged bribery, weapons arrangements or other pleaded misconduct occurred. The claims, any witnesses' allegations, and the company's denials must be read separately from adjudicated facts. MTN's 30 April 2025 market statement disputed the allegations and noted that merits had not been tested in court at that date; that statement is the company's position, not an independent resolution. Check for subsequent judgments before characterising the dispute's current status. Added 21 October 2026.
