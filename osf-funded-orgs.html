Netwerk Leaks  |  Funding Infrastructure Investigation

The Open Society
Funding Network
in South Africa

How three distinct funding systems, OSF, Omidyar/Luminate and USAID, have funded overlapping parts of South Africa's civil-society infrastructure. Sometimes independently. Sometimes through shared recipients. In several cases through shared or pooled funding programmes.

This investigation does not argue that OSF, Omidyar/Luminate and USAID are one organisation or operate under common control. It documents how a major private foundation, a second private philanthropic network and a U.S. government development agency have funded overlapping parts of South Africa's civil-society infrastructure, and where those funding systems intersect through joint or pooled vehicles.

Context

Three Different Funders. Overlapping Fields.

They have different legal structures, different governance, different stated purposes. The investigative interest is where they converge.

Private Philanthropy, USA

Open Society Foundations / OSF-SA

Founded by George Soros. OSF established its South African foundation in 1993, during South Africa’s transition to democracy. Its own 2025 retrospective says it invested $238,635,789 in South Africa from 1997–2023, supporting roughly 1,000 organisations and individuals over its 30-year history. OSF's 2021–23 consolidation wound up the national foundation, but OSF says support for South Africa continues through the unified global structure.

Focus: institution building, public-interest law, constitutionalism, accountability, independent media, gender and health, criminal justice, advocacy, long-term/core funding.
Private Philanthropy, USA

Omidyar Network / Luminate

Created by eBay founder Pierre Omidyar and Pam Omidyar. In 2018, the governance/civic engagement portfolio became Luminate, a standalone philanthropic organisation. South Africa is explicitly named as a Luminate priority country alongside Kenya and Nigeria. Omidyar sub-entities include Imaginable Futures (education) and Flourish Ventures (fintech).

Focus: digital media, investigative journalism, civic technology, open data, fact-checking, information integrity, citizen mobilisation, concentrates heavily in the information/media infrastructure layer where OSF's portfolio also operates.
U.S. Government Aid, Public

USAID

Unlike the first two, USAID is government, funded by the United States government and subject to congressional oversight. Its South African democracy and governance programmes have historically sought to strengthen civil society, civil-society/government relationships, and accountability. USAID does not appear to mirror Omidyar/Luminate's South African portfolio exactly, its strongest overlap occurs through implementation and through the Making All Voices Count (MAVC) joint programme.

Focus: governance/accountability projects, health, gender/GBV, violence prevention, civil-society strengthening, technology-for-governance, intersects selectively with the OSF/Omidyar ecosystem.

Why compare three organisations with different structures? The key finding is not that these funders secretly coordinate. It is that distinct public and private funders repeatedly appear in the same sectors, programmes and recipient ecosystem, sometimes through direct grants and sometimes through joint or pooled vehicles. When two foundations and a government agency participate in the same funding programme (as documented with MAVC), the distinction between "separate donors funding the same org" and "co-investors in shared infrastructure" becomes material.

The evidence proves co-funding, common vehicles, overlapping recipient institutions and shared fields of activity. It does not, by itself, prove that donors jointly dictate editorial positions, that one donor controls another, or that receiving money means a particular output was donor-directed. Establishing that would require grant agreements, scopes of work, restricted-vs-core conditions, programme memoranda and board minutes.

Funding Architecture

How the Money Moves

Three tiers: the funding sources, the formal bridges built between them, and the operational organisations that receive money through multiple channels.

What "direct" vs "pooled" means: A direct grant appears in the recipient's accounts under the funder's name. A pooled grant routes through an intermediary vehicle (CF, SAMIP, MAVC), so the recipient's accounts carry the vehicle name rather than the original donor. Both matter for network mapping; they are not equivalent findings.

Core findings

What the Investigation Found

$238M, The Official Total

OSF's 30-year retrospective (March 2025) confirms $238,635,789 invested 1997–2023, excluding the original $15M endowment, supporting roughly 1,000 organisations and individuals. This is the first time OSF has disclosed the full cumulative figure.

Stacked Funding, The Core Discovery

The Constitutionalism Fund is not replacing direct OSF funding. It is an additional funding rail stacked on top. ProBono.Org received CF R2M + OSF R1.3M simultaneously in 2018. amaBhungane had three channels simultaneously in FY2023: OSF-SA R812,942 + CF-AOSI R842,211 + Luminate R2,124,677.

Formal Bridges, SAMIP and MAVC

SAMIP was deliberately built as a joint OSF-SA + Omidyar funding mechanism, managed by MDIF. Making All Voices Count ($47M programme) documented USAID + Omidyar + DFID/Sida, with early programme documents also listing OSF as a contributor alongside the four other donors. These are not coincidental overlaps; they are shared programmes.

OSF Built the Institutional Backbone

OSF's retrospective describes increasing work as a "multi-sectoral connector, convener and commissioner of research", not just a grantmaker. It explicitly describes its original mission as institution-building: establishing autonomous civil-society institutions, legal access infrastructure and independent media simultaneously.

Omidyar/Luminate Overlaps the Media/Civic-Tech Layer

Omidyar/Luminate concentrates almost exactly where OSF's work becomes digital: media innovation, civic technology, digital publishing, fact-checking, information integrity, online mobilisation. Africa Check, OpenUp, amandla.mobi and MMA/Moxii all carry both OSF and Omidyar/Luminate funding.

USAID Intersects Selectively

USAID's strongest direct overlaps are CSVR (cooperative agreement AID-674-A-14-00016), Sonke Gender Justice (APC programme, multiple grants) and PMG (historically: OSF + Royal Danish Embassy + USAID documented simultaneously). Through MAVC, USAID directly co-invested in the tech/accountability space typically associated with Omidyar and OSF.

Shared Vehicles

Where the Funders Formally Meet

The most significant finding is not coincidental overlap. It is where distinct funders deliberately built or participated in joint funding programmes.

SAMIP, South Africa Media Innovation Program
OSF-SA + Omidyar Network | 2017–present | Managed by MDIF

SAMIP was not an NGO that separately happened to win money from two foundations. MDIF's original 2017 announcement is explicit: SAMIP was initiated by OSF-SA, funded by Omidyar Network, and managed by MDIF. Its purpose: funding and capacity-building for independent South African media, digital media, new voices, underserved communities and sustainable business models.

OSF-SA ──────────────┐ ├──► SAMIP ──► MDIF manages Omidyar Network ────┘ │ ├── Daily Maverick ├── Digital/community media ├── Youth media ventures └── Media innovation projects

After Omidyar's governance portfolio became Luminate, SAMIP identified its funders as OSF-SA and Luminate. The formal bridge between the two foundations into the South African information ecosystem is documented from the programme's own materials.

MAVC, Making All Voices Count
USAID + Omidyar + DFID/Sida | $47M programme | OSF documented in early materials

MAVC issued 178 grants and operated in South Africa and other countries. Its current archived "About" page names DFID + USAID + Sida + Omidyar Network. But earlier official programme documents, including the MAVC Strategy Synthesis, explicitly add Open Society Foundations as a fifth contributor.

DFID ─────────────────┐ USAID ($10M confirmed) ─┤ Sida ──────────────── ─┤──► MAVC ($47M) ──► 178 grants Omidyar Network ─────────┤ incl. SA OSF* ─────────────────── ┘ *contemporary programme documents

Hivos's 2013 annual report records OSF contributing at least €1.5M; Omidyar €3.5M. Hivos's 2014 report calls OSF a "key architect" of MAVC. This means amandla.mobi and OpenUp sit inside a programme where OSF, Omidyar and USAID themselves were all documented funders, not merely "three donors who each separately funded the same NGO."

Note: the current MAVC archive omitting OSF conflicts with the programme's earlier documents. The most plausible explanations include different programme phases, commitments versus final donor accounting, or website simplification. The earlier primary documents are the stronger sources.
Constitutionalism Fund
OSF + Ford Foundation + Atlantic Philanthropies | $29M | 2015–2025

A formal joint fund, not three donors separately funding the same grantees. CF's own homepage confirms: "CF oversees a US$29 million joint investment made in 2015, established by Atlantic Philanthropies, Ford Foundation and Open Society Foundation." OSF-SA initially administered the fund; AOSI was fiscal sponsor. Later, Ford took administration and the New Venture Fund became fiscal sponsor as the "Ford Foundation Dignity and Justice Fund – South Africa Constitutionalism Initiative."

[2015] OSF + Atlantic + Ford → CF pooled vehicle → OSF-SA administration → AOSI fiscal sponsor [Later] Ford takes administration → NVF (New Venture Fund) fiscal sponsor → "Ford Foundation Dignity & Justice Fund – SA Constitutionalism Initiative" [2026] amandla.mobi still lists CF via NVF as funder GroundUp accounts: R1.9M (2023/24) via NVF

Its first cohort (2016) was 12 organisations pre-selected from existing Atlantic/OSF trusted grantees. CF's own website says "more than 200 organisations received funding", the 65 named partners are the visible surface only.

OSF–USAID: Two Directly Documented Nodes
Direct, not through a shared programme

CSVR: Both OSF-SA and USAID appear on CSVR's own donor page. USAID's OIG audited USAID resources managed by CSVR under cooperative agreement AID-674-A-14-00016 for its regional torture-victim rehabilitation programme. This is one of the cleaner OSF–USAID overlaps in the database.

Sonke Gender Justice: OSF-SA gave R1.22M (2023) and R1.55M (2022), confirmed in Sonke's audited accounts. USAID's APC programme separately funded Sonke to provide gender and family-planning technical assistance to USAID missions and implementing partners, with further Lesotho work implemented through Pact.

PMG: An OSF study on open data in South Africa records PMG's then-current donors as Open Society Foundation, Royal Danish Embassy and USAID, historically documented in a contemporaneous OSF document.

Key structural discovery

Stacked Funding

The Constitutionalism Fund is not replacing direct OSF funding. It is an additional funding rail. Multiple organisations receive both direct OSF grants and CF grants simultaneously, confirmed from audited financial statements.

Documented Simultaneous Funding, from Audited Accounts

These amounts are drawn from recipients' own published audited financial statements, not estimates.

ProBono.Org (2018): CF R2,000,000 + OSF-SA R1,303,000 [simultaneous] ProBono.Org (2019): CF R1,500,000 + OSF R800,000 [simultaneous] amaBhungane (FY2023): OSF-SA R812,942 + CF-AOSI R842,211 + Luminate R2,124,677 PARI (2025 brochure): CF listed + Ford Foundation listed + OSF South Africa listed Centre for Child Law: CF R700,000 + Direct OSF R750,000 [2016 annual report] Open Secrets (2023): OSF-HRI R1,417,000 + OSF-SA R1,524,000 + Luminate R3,671,463

Qualification: simultaneous funding does not mean the grants funded the same activity, or that any donor directed the other's output. It demonstrates parallel funding channels through distinct but overlapping funding channels operating concurrently at a single recipient.

Case study, information ecosystem

The Information Infrastructure Chain

Within the broader OSF funding network, one specific chain within the information ecosystem is the most densely documented. The following traces how OSF- and Luminate-funded institutions connect from academic framework through fact-checking to content moderation, and where the Afrikaans-language press sits outside this chain.

01
OSF-Adjacent Academic

Academic Definition, Prof Herman Wasserman (Stellenbosch University)

Leading academic definer of "disinformation" in the African context. OSF + Stellenbosch funding. Academic frameworks adopted internationally, cited in IFCN-affiliated work and referenced by Africa Check.

02
OSF 12% + Luminate 8% + Gates 13% + AFP Foundation

Africa Check (Wits, Johannesburg)

Founded by Peter Cunliffe-Jones while on AFP payroll; housed at Wits; AFP Foundation provided seed funding. Partners with News24 and Daily Maverick. Cited by CNN, PBS, BBC, Al Jazeera, Wikipedia. Omidyar Network + OSF gave a joint $500,000 grant for automated fact-checking tools in 2017.

03
Code governs Meta content moderation

IFCN (International Fact-Checking Network)

Peter Cunliffe-Jones co-authored the IFCN Code of Principles. Meta historically used IFCN-certified third-party fact-checkers to moderate content internationally, including in South Africa. In January 2025, Meta ended this programme in the United States and replaced it with Community Notes; the international transition has been separate and is proceeding on a different timeline.

04
International, outside SA

Meta (Facebook / Instagram)

Partners with IFCN-accredited fact-checkers in markets outside the U.S., including South Africa. Posts flagged by fact-checkers under this system see reduced reach. The specific effect on Afrikaner-related content requires the Real411 complaint dataset, which has not yet been fully analysed.

05
Circular citation, OSF network

Daily Maverick + News24 + Mail & Guardian

OSF + Luminate funded. Africa Check cites them as primary sources. Adri Basson (OSF-SA helped establish amaBhungane, where Basson was a co-founder) serves as News24 Editor-in-Chief. NED paid M&G Adamela Trust $355,200 (2020–21).

06
Structural, not policy

International Citation, Language Silo

Africa Check is cited by CNN, PBS, BBC, Snopes, Al Jazeera and Wikipedia. Our mapping to date has identified substantially fewer documented connections between Afrikaans-language outlets and international wire services, fact-checkers, or media training pipelines, compared with English-language South African media. This structural difference deserves further systematic mapping before definitive conclusions are drawn.

07
OSF-funded via MMA, confirmed

Real411 (operated by Media Monitoring Africa / Moxii Africa)

OSF funded ($200,000 in 2023; MMA Director William Bird: "We do indeed receive funding from the Open Society Foundation," Nov 2020). Platform for reporting online content as disinformation. The specific demographic breakdown of complaints has not yet been published by MMA.

Editorial qualification: The chain above traces documented funding relationships and an institutional pathway. It does not prove that Africa Check's editorial decisions are directed by its funders, that Meta's content moderation in South Africa is controlled by OSF, or that Real411 complaints are predominantly about Afrikaner content (the complaint data has not yet been publicly released or independently analysed). Each of those questions requires a different level of evidence.

Verification Codes

  • OSF-D: Direct OSF / OSF-SA / OSISA grant
  • CF-2: Constitutionalism Fund (second-degree pooled)
  • CF-AOSI: CF via AOSI fiscal sponsor (early phase)
  • CF-NVF: CF via New Venture Fund (later phase)
  • STACKED: CF + direct OSF simultaneously from audited accounts
  • OMD: Omidyar/Luminate/IF/Flourish direct

Verification Standard

  • VERIFIED: 2+ independent primary sources
  • HISTORICAL: Confirmed from historical reports or archives
  • PROJECT-LEVEL: One specific project confirmed; full relationship unclear
  • PARTIALLY VERIFIED: 1 primary source or strong network indicator
  • Amounts listed as "not disclosed" are genuinely absent from public records

What This Proves, and Doesn't

  • Proves: co-funding, common vehicles, overlapping recipients, shared fields
  • Does not prove: donors jointly dictate editorial positions
  • Does not prove: one donor secretly controls another
  • Does not prove: output was donor-directed
  • Those questions require: grant agreements, scopes of work, restricted vs core conditions, programme memoranda, board minutes
