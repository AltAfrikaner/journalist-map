Second-Degree Conduit

The Constitutionalism Fund

The $29M Constitutionalism Fund achieves donor-attribution attenuation: a recipient's annual report carries "Constitutionalism Fund" rather than "Open Society Foundations." The investigation classification is pooled-fund intermediation — the architecture is disclosed, the attribution is diffused.

CF Architecture & Money Trail

$29M total — Atlantic Philanthropies $10M + Open Society Foundations $10M + Ford Foundation $9M.

[2015] OSF + Atlantic + Ford → CF pooled vehicle → OSF-SA administration → AOSI fiscal sponsor → South African grantees [Later] Ford takes administration → NVF becomes fiscal sponsor → "Ford Foundation Dignity and Justice Fund – South Africa Constitutionalism Initiative" → South African grantees [2026] amandla.mobi still lists CF via NVF as current funder GroundUp audited accounts: R1.9M (2023/24) + R1.8M (2024/25) via NVF

First cohort (2016): 12 organisations pre-selected from existing Atlantic/OSF grantees — Black Sash, Equal Education, LRC, CALS, Centre for Law and Society, Centre for Child Law, LHR, SECTION27, SERI, Ndifuna Ukwazi, ProBono.Org, SJC. Source: LRC 2015/16 Annual Report.

Universe: CF's own website states "more than 200 organisations received funding." The 65 named partners on its public page are the visible surface only.

The Stacked Funding Discovery

CF is not replacing direct OSF funding. It is an additional funding rail. Multiple organisations receive both simultaneously.

Omidyar Group — Secondary

Omidyar Group Funded Organisations

Includes Omidyar Network Fund, Luminate, Imaginable Futures (education), and Flourish Ventures (fintech). All Omidyar entities are funded by Pierre and Pam Omidyar. Amounts verified against Form 990-PF filings where available. The full architecture — including Luminate's position inside the IEC referral pipeline — is in the Omidyar Network investigation.

Note: Omidyar as USAID Replacement. When USAID was defunded and dismantled in 2025, Luminate quietly continued paying the same South African NGOs that USAID had previously supported. The Omidyar network did not expand into this space by accident — it had been positioning there since 2017. The full USAID South Africa investigation documents $1.2 billion in SA civil-society funding from 2008–2025. What USAID funded publicly, Omidyar now funds privately. The accountability gap between the two vehicles is total: USAID grants require public disclosure; Luminate grants require only what appears in annual 990-PF filings and recipient disclosures.

The Church World Service (CWS investigation) runs an adjacent but structurally separate architecture — the USRAP refugee resettlement programme — under a sole-source US State Department contract. Together, OSF, Omidyar, USAID, and CWS represent four distinct funding channels converging on South African civil society simultaneously.

Verification Standard

  • VERIFIED: 2+ independent primary sources
  • HISTORICAL VERIFIED: Confirmed from OSF historical reports or archives
  • PROJECT-LEVEL VERIFIED: One specific project confirmed; full relationship unclear
  • PARTIALLY VERIFIED: 1 primary source or strong network indicator
  • STACKED: Confirmed from audited recipient accounts — both direct OSF + CF in same year

Anti-Hallucination Rules

  • Minimum two independent sources for every VERIFIED claim
  • No invented grant amounts — unknown amounts stated as such
  • Entity distinctions maintained (ALA ≠ ALU; CF grantee ≠ direct OSF grantee)
  • Stacked funding documented only from recipient audited accounts
  • STACKED classification requires concurrent grants in same accounting year
  • No amounts extrapolated from partial disclosures

Funding Codes

  • OSF-D: Direct OSF / OSF-SA / OSISA grant
  • CF-2: Constitutionalism Fund (second-degree pooled)
  • CF-AOSI: CF via AOSI fiscal sponsor (early)
  • CF-NVF: CF via New Venture Fund (late phase)
  • STACKED: CF + direct OSF simultaneously (audited)
  • OMD: Omidyar / Luminate / IF / Flourish direct
