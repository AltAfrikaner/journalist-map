Core findings

What the Investigation Found

Cross-referencing donor filings, recipient accounts and grantmaker directories identifies relationships worth investigating, but this is a selected register, not a previously complete census or proof of common donor control. The 73 OSF-linked rows include grantmaking bodies and a programme, and the 35 Omidyar-family rows mix grants with investments. Each finding links to a related investigation, while row-level verification remains open.

Stacked Funding — Core Discovery

Parallel funding is documented in selected years, but grants and donations must be distinguished. ProBono.Org's 2018 annual report (p. 38) lists a R2m Constitutionalism Fund grant alongside an OSF-SA donation of R1,303,288. amaBhungane's FY2023 supplementary income schedule (p. 19) identifies R3,779,830 across AOSI/CF, direct OSF-SA and separately governed Luminate. That supplementary schedule is explicitly unaudited. These dated receipts do not establish that every donor operated as a single network, or directed any story.

Second-Degree Conduit

The Constitutionalism Fund

The Constitutionalism Fund is an identified pooled funding arrangement involving Open Society Foundations, Atlantic Philanthropies and the Ford Foundation. Its 2015 launch announcements described $25 million; the Fund's later institutional account reports $29 million for the overall programme. Citing the pooled Fund rather than an individual contributor is not proof of deliberate donor concealment. See the source reconciliation on the OSF investigation page.

CF Architecture & Money Trail

$29M attributed programme figure — the Fund's later account describes $29m, after a $25m collective launch announcement in February 2015. Open Society Foundations was the recorded recipient of a $10m Atlantic grant; it is not evidence of a further separate $10m contribution by OSF. The former asserted $10m Atlantic + $10m OSF + $9m Ford donor-by-donor split is UNVERIFIED until original contributor ledgers are reconciled without double counting pass-through grants.

[2015] OSF + Atlantic + Ford → CF pooled vehicle → OSF-SA administration → AOSI fiscal sponsor → South African grantees [Later] Ford takes administration → NVF becomes fiscal sponsor → "Ford Foundation Dignity and Justice Fund – South Africa Constitutionalism Initiative" → South African grantees [2026] amandla.mobi lists CF via NVF as a 2026 funder (self-report; cash date unverified) [2024] GroundUp donor register: R1.9M (February) + R1.8M (December) via NVF These are dated donor disclosures, NOT two audited-year cash totals

Grant decisions and donor authority: The 5 February 2015 Ford/Atlantic/OSF launch announcement describes a local selection panel chaired by former Constitutional Court justice Yvonne Mokgoro and says its recommendations went to the donors for final ratification. The Fund's later published grantmaking procedure instead describes committee scoring, a fiscal host and contractual compliance. The original donor ratification right is a documented form of influence over grant selection; it does not establish donor instructions to a grantee about a particular article, court case or platform decision. Whether specific contracts restricted editorial decisions remains UNVERIFIED.

GroundUp accounting distinction: The R1.9m (February 2024) and R1.8m (December 2024) figures are listed in GroundUp's donor disclosures (updated 6 March 2026), against separate financial years. GroundUp calls its 2023/24 and 2024/25 financial statements independently reviewed, not audited. Its 2025 signed statements, independent reviewer's report and note 7 expressly disclaim an audit opinion and record R1,109,678 of unspent deferred income for the NVF constitutionalism initiative at 28 February 2025. A year-end deferred balance is not a new award, nor the same quantity as the R1.8m donor disclosure. The 2026 amandla.mobi funder listing is that recipient's self-report and does not prove a 2026 cash transfer to GroundUp.

First cohort (2016): 12 organisations pre-selected from existing Atlantic/OSF grantees — Black Sash, Equal Education, LRC, CALS, Centre for Law and Society, Centre for Child Law, LHR, SECTION27, SERI, Ndifuna Ukwazi, ProBono.Org, SJC. Source: LRC 2015/16 Annual Report.

Universe: The Constitutionalism Fund’s own overview says “more than 60” funded partners, while its partner-stories page refers to “more than 200 organisations” receiving support. Those figures may cover different categories or periods, but a reconciled unique-recipient roster is not established here. The exact recipient total remains UNVERIFIED; 65 visible names are not the entire funding universe.

Omidyar Group — Secondary

Omidyar Group Funded Organisations

The directory groups Omidyar Network-related funds, Luminate, Imaginable Futures and Flourish Ventures; their legal entities, donor funding and equity investments are not interchangeable. A venture round's total syndicate financing must not be attributed to a single investor or reported as a charitable grant. Original Form 990-PF entries and recipient statements still need matching by legal entity, year and amount. The Omidyar Network investigation examines institutional links and IEC-related hypotheses; a grant or investment alone does not prove instructions about individual IEC referrals.

Evidence review — USAID transition and private donors. USAID's independent operations were largely dismantled in 2025, but selected federal awards and responsibilities were transferred to the US Department of State rather than automatically ending. The State Department Inspector General (July 2026) documents transferred foreign-assistance awards; the US Government Accountability Office (30 September 2026) records substantial terminations and incomplete award-level data. Luminate's grants and publicly accessible tax returns and audited financial statements predate this transition. Overlapping recipients raise legitimate questions about funding continuity, but the assertion that Omidyar/Luminate intentionally replaced USAID in South Africa remains UNVERIFIED until matched award-by-award, including donor, intermediary, payment date, amount and purpose. The claim that private grantmaking has no disclosure obligations is inaccurate: applicable US private-foundation filings are public, though their detail and timing differ from federal award records.

The Church World Service investigation concerns a different US refugee-resettlement programme. Merely identifying four organisations operating in the same country does not establish coordinated command or a common agenda. This directory invites scrutiny of each transaction while retaining these institutional distinctions.

Row 35 correction (littlefish): The original source row's “$9.5M Series A (2026)” means the whole investment syndicate, not $9.5m from Flourish Ventures. Partech's 24 March 2026 announcement and independent business reporting identify Partech as lead with TLcom, Flourish and Proparco participating. Flourish's precise investment is UNVERIFIED. The table's inherited “Confirmed Amount” heading is a legacy label, not an independent award-level certification; its 35 funding rows remain subject to source-by-source audit.

Verification Standard

  • VERIFIED (legacy dataset status): a label assigned in the original directory, not certification by this editorial audit. Before final release, confirm the dated award with two non-duplicative records, distinguishing donor commitments from recipient payments.
  • HISTORICAL VERIFIED: Confirmed from OSF historical reports or archives
  • PROJECT-LEVEL VERIFIED: One specific project confirmed; full relationship unclear
  • PARTIALLY VERIFIED: 1 primary source or strong network indicator
  • STACKED (legacy tag): Concurrent direct OSF and CF receipts can occur in specific accounting years. Confirm dated amounts, entity identity and whether the detailed grant schedule is inside or outside the independent auditor's assurance scope.

Anti-Hallucination Rules

  • Release target: two independent sources for every re-certified VERIFIED claim; entries below this standard remain UNVERIFIED as to amount, date or purpose until checked.
  • No invented grant amounts — unknown amounts stated as such
  • Entity distinctions maintained (ALA ≠ ALU; CF grantee ≠ direct OSF grantee)
  • Identify whether each stacked receipt appears in audited financial statements or in a separately unaudited supplementary schedule; do not represent both as independently assured.
  • STACKED re-certification requires same-period receipts or commitments distinguished by donor, legal recipient, accounting basis and documentation; mixed years must not be combined.
  • No amounts extrapolated from partial disclosures

Funding directory · Editorial evidence check · October 2026

How to read the funding directory without mistaking links for instructions

A funding register is strongest when every entry identifies the legal recipient, grantmaker, amount, grant year, currency, and original disclosure. It becomes misleading when contributions from different years are summed as though they form a single annual programme, or when a grant to one legal entity is attributed to every institution sharing a donor or founder. This page maps identified relationships, not a complete audit of every South African grant. Numbers and relationship types should be checked against the displayed source for each row.

Funded does not mean directed

Africa Check's own funding page publishes funders and rounded income shares by year, explains its commitment to donor diversity, and states that funding partners do not direct editorial judgments. That last statement is the organisation's policy claim rather than an independent guarantee. The IFCN's published Code of Principles provides an outside standard requiring signatories to identify funders and maintain independence. Questions about a particular rating or undisclosed conflict still require the specific article and a date-matched funding record.

The percentages sometimes quoted for the Open Society Foundations, Gates Foundation and Luminate should not be used as timeless current shares. An individual donor's share can change substantially by reporting year and as the denominator changes. The funding table's date and source must accompany any percentage. As of October 2026, Africa Check lists a current set of partners, including an OSF-supported collaborative funding arrangement. That supports a relationship, but does not prove a particular donor ordered a correction, suppressed a post or paid an individual journalist.

IFCN certification and Meta's decision are different things

The IFCN's explanation of platform relationships says several technology companies have used verified signatory status as a necessary but insufficient condition for participating in certain fact-checking programmes. Signatory accreditation is not a universal moderation command. Individual organisations make their own arrangements with platforms, which then apply their published policies. Meta's own election-safeguards directory lists Africa Check as a contact in South Africa, but that listing alone does not reveal a specific editorial decision, content rating, account penalty or the audience affected.

Meta's 7 January 2025 announcement described labels and reduced distribution under its former third-party fact-checking system, and an initially US-only switch towards Community Notes. Other regions and later years require separate policy checks. Without a case-level rating and platform action notice, an assertion that OSF money caused Meta to penalise Afrikaner farm-attack reporting remains UNVERIFIED. This is the evidence threshold a serious investigation should apply rather than substituting a relationship map for a causal proof.

Language claims, pooled funds and missing figures

Nor can this directory establish that no Afrikaans-language outlet has an international partnership. The map only shows entries added to this project's data. An absent edge in a dataset is not evidence that no external relationship exists. Any broad claim about Afrikaner representation needs a defined outlet sample, dated rosters, cited collaboration records and voluntarily disclosed language proficiency where relevant. Those details remain UNVERIFIED here.

The Constitutionalism Fund is a separate pooled funding vehicle. Ford's 2015 launch announcement identified $25 million, whereas the Fund's later account states $29 million over the programme. Those are dated disclosures, not proof that the $4 million difference was concealed or that recipients colluded to hide donor names. A grant-by-grant schedule would be needed to reconcile the increase. The primary records and the fuller distinction are also linked on the OSF network investigation.

Editorial finding: Donor influence and disclosure requirements are legitimate matters for scrutiny. The records establish specific grants, partnerships and organisational structures. They do not by themselves establish editorial instructions, individual payroll, a universal suppression chain, or the absence of international Afrikaans media connections. Claims beyond the documentary link must be presented as inference or UNVERIFIED until additional records substantiate them.
