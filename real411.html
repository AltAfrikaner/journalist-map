Real411: The Foreign-Funded South African Gateway to Meta, Google and TikTok

A Johannesburg-based civil society trust manages South Africa's official public-complaints portal for online misinformation. Through a formal cooperation agreement, its reviewed complaints reach Meta, Google and TikTok as prioritised escalations. Its operator has received at least $825,000 in identified foreign philanthropic funding since 2019. By 2026, it co-leads a formal election-information coalition with Africa Check and the South African National Editors Forum.

DOCUMENTED FINDING: A privately operated, foreign-funded civil-society complaints system holds an official role in South Africa's electoral-information architecture and a prioritised escalation route into major technology platforms whose own policies determine the ultimate moderation outcome. The funding connections are documented. What remains unestablished is whether these escalation pathways produce viewpoint-asymmetric outcomes.

What Is Real411? What Does It Do?

Real411 is a website where any South African can report an online post they believe is false, harmful or spreading lies. It was built by a Johannesburg non-profit organisation called Media Monitoring Africa (MMA) and launched in 2019 together with the Electoral Commission of South Africa (IEC) -- the independent body that runs the country's elections.

When you submit a complaint, a small panel of volunteer experts reviews it. If they find it crosses a legal or ethical threshold, the complaint can travel through a formal system all the way to Facebook, Google or TikTok. The platform then decides what to do: remove the post, add a warning label, delist it from search results -- or take no action at all.

That mechanism -- private organisation, official status, formal tech-platform access -- is what this investigation examines. It is not about whether Real411 has done something wrong. It is about mapping what it is, how it is funded, who controls it and what questions the available evidence does and does not yet answer.

The Basic Idea

How Real411 Works in Four Steps

📷
Step 1
You See a Post
You come across something online that looks false, misleading or harmful. It could be on Facebook, TikTok, X (formerly Twitter), WhatsApp or any other platform.
📋
Step 2
You Report It
You submit a complaint on Real411's website. You describe what you saw and why you think it is harmful. Anyone in South Africa can do this for free.
🔍
Step 3
Experts Review It
Three volunteers from a pool of 30-plus reviewers -- lawyers, journalists, digital specialists -- assess your complaint. They were trained at the University of the Witwatersrand.
⚖

A Decision Is Made
The result ranges from no action at all to an urgent referral to Facebook, Google, TikTok, the Human Rights Commission or the police -- depending on what the reviewers found.

Why Does This Investigation Exist?

Real411 is not run by the government. It is run by a private trust. That trust has received substantial foreign funding. And through a formal agreement, it has a privileged escalation route into the world's largest social media platforms -- one that an ordinary member of the public reporting the same post does not have.

None of that is secret. All of it is documented in public records, official press releases and tax filings. This page puts it all in one place and asks the questions the documents do not yet answer: who reviews what, what gets referred, and do any particular kinds of content or viewpoints end up referred more than others?

Those questions are open. This page documents what is established and names what is not.

Step by Step

The Journey of a Complaint

The flowchart below shows what happens after you submit a complaint to Real411. Each stage is drawn from official IEC and MMA documents.

Stage 1 — Anyone
Public submits a complaint on real411.org.za
Free, open to any South African. Covers four types: disinformation, hate speech, incitement to violence, harassment of journalists.
Stage 2 — Volunteer Reviewers
Three experts from the 30-plus pool assess the complaint independently
Volunteers with legal, digital and media expertise. Trained at the Mandela Institute of Law, University of the Witwatersrand. Their identities are not publicly listed. They have no binding legal powers over any party.
Stage 3 — MMA Secretariat
Media Monitoring Africa reviews the assessment and determines next steps
The outcome and any referral decision is made here. All outcomes are published in a public archive. The complainant can track progress and appeal decisions.
Stage 4 — Possible Paths
What can happen next
The outcome depends entirely on what the reviewers found. Here are all the documented possibilities:
No action taken Content does not meet any threshold. Complaint closed.
Counter-narrative Real411 publishes factual information. No platform or authority involved.
Press Ombud Referred to South Africa's media self-regulation body.
Platform notified IEC tells the platform (Meta / Google / TikTok). Platform reviews under its own rules and may: remove the post, add a warning label, delist from search, or take no action.
SAHRC referred Referred to the South African Human Rights Commission -- a constitutional body that can alert police and issue public statements.
SAPS / courts Incitement or criminal content may be referred to the South African Police Service or legal proceedings initiated.
Stage 5 — Public Record
All outcomes published. Right of appeal available.
Every complaint decision is publicly accessible. Appeals are overseen by Justice Zak Yacoob, a former acting Deputy Chief Justice of South Africa's Constitutional Court. The Real411 Digital Complaints Committee has no legally binding authority over any platform, authority or person at any stage.

The Critical Difference: Prioritised Access

When an ordinary member of the public reports a post directly on Facebook or TikTok, they enter a general queue with millions of other reports. When a Real411-assessed complaint is forwarded by the IEC to those same platforms, the platforms have formally committed to give it priority attention -- to "acknowledge and swiftly process the notification." The platforms still decide the outcome under their own rules. But they decide it from a queue no ordinary citizen can access. That priority status was created by a formal cooperation agreement between MMA, the IEC, and the technology companies.

The Organisation

Who Runs Real411?

Real411 is owned and operated by Media Monitoring Africa (MMA), which rebranded as Moxii Africa in 2026. MMA was founded in 1993 to monitor how South African broadcasters covered the country's first democratic elections. It has grown since then into a broad media-accountability organisation running more than 40 programmes. Real411 is its most publicly visible product.

Important: What the IEC Is and What It Is Not

The IEC -- Electoral Commission of South Africa -- is a Chapter 9 constitutional institution. Chapter 9 of South Africa's Constitution establishes a set of independent bodies designed to support democracy. The IEC is one of them. It is constitutionally required to be independent and impartial. It is accountable to Parliament, not to the ruling party or any other political organisation.

In plain language: the IEC is not an ANC government body. It is a state body specifically designed to operate independently of whichever party is in government. Whether that independence works as intended in practice is a separate and legitimate question. It remains a factual error to describe the IEC as an ANC enforcement arm.

Values-Based, Not Neutral

In November 2020, MMA Director William Bird was asked directly whether Real411 was biased. His answer is worth reading in full:

"Are we biased? Yes, we are. We do not seek to hide it, we own it and are clear about it on the Real411 site. We don't believe in a fabled objectivity, but we do believe in human rights, in fairness, balance, accuracy and the SA Constitution." William Bird, MMA Director. Daily Maverick, 15 November 2020. Verified.

He is not claiming to favour a political party. He is saying Real411 applies values -- human rights, the Constitution -- rather than claiming a neutral "we have no view" stance. That is a legitimate philosophical position. The question it raises for an investigator is different: when assessments are made through a normative lens rather than a purely procedural one, how do we know viewpoint-neutral outcomes are actually being produced? The complaint distribution data in the public archive is the place to test this. That analysis has not been published.

A Documented Contradiction in the 2026 Framework

Moxii Africa's own 2026 Local Government Elections page contains two statements that do not agree with each other. The page first says Moxii Africa and the IEC "have signed a Framework of Cooperation, committing to protect the integrity of the LGE 2026." The same page then says "The Framework of Cooperation is still under negotiation and will be linked here soon."

If it is signed, the full document should be accessible under South Africa's Promotion of Access to Information Act (PAIA). If it is still being negotiated, the operative rules for 2026 have not been finalised. This investigation has filed two PAIA requests with the IEC specifically targeting this document. No public copy has been published as of September 2026.

Follow the Money

Who Privately Funds Real411?

The short answer

Real411 is not government-funded. It is operated by a private non-profit trust, Media Monitoring Africa (MMA) -- now Moxii Africa -- that is funded primarily by two major American private philanthropic networks and a German state-funded political foundation.

The two biggest identified private funders are:

1. The Omidyar Group / Luminate -- Pierre Omidyar, the French-American billionaire who founded eBay, channels his philanthropy through a network of organisations. One of them is Luminate. Luminate gave MMA at least $465,000 between 2020 and 2022. The Omidyar Network Fund gave MMA $160,000 in 2019. That is a minimum of $625,000 from the Omidyar network in four years. This hub has a full investigation dedicated to the Omidyar network and its South African reach. See the Omidyar investigation

2. Open Society Foundations (OSF) -- founded by investor George Soros. OSF gave MMA $200,000 in 2023 alone. OSF also funds Africa Check, the other fact-checking organisation that sits inside the same PIIE coalition as Real411. See the OSF investigation

3. Friedrich Ebert Stiftung (FES) -- this one is different. FES is not a private funder. It is a German political foundation funded overwhelmingly by the German federal and state governments. It has also historically supported the ANC. Its SA grant quantum to MMA is not publicly disclosed.

No public ownership record exists for the trust itself. MMA is a non-benefit trust registered with the Master of the High Court in Johannesburg -- not with South Africa's companies regulator (CIPC). Trustee names and the trust deed are not on any public website. Obtaining them requires a formal access request to the Master's Office.

The grant records below come from primary sources -- mainly US Internal Revenue Service Form 990-PF tax filings, which are publicly available legal documents that foundations are required to file every year listing their grants. These are not allegations. They are public record.

Documenting these funding relationships does not mean the funders directed individual Real411 complaint decisions. It means the organisation operating Real411 has received substantial financial support from private and state-funded foreign bodies, some of which also fund other organisations operating in the same information-integrity space.

The Friedrich Ebert Stiftung: A German Political Foundation with Historic ANC Ties

The Friedrich Ebert Stiftung (FES) is a German political foundation funded primarily by the German federal and state governments, with an annual budget of approximately 200 million euros. FES's own documentation records its relationship with South Africa in plain terms: "The Friedrich-Ebert-Stiftung shares a long common history with the African National Congress and the Congress of South African Trade Unions. From the 1980s, FES supported the ANC in exile." The international development information platform Devex records that FES "works in close cooperation with the ANC."

In plain language: German government money flows through a foundation that has explicitly supported the ANC since the 1980s and that continues to work alongside ANC-aligned civil society organisations -- to fund the trust that operates Real411. Every one of those facts is from FES's own or publicly verifiable documentation. The SA office during 2021 to 2025 was directed by Sebastian Sperling, who left for the FES Argentina office in October 2025. His replacement had not been publicly identified at the time of publication.

The 2026 Information Architecture

PIIE: Where the Pieces Come Together

For the 2026 Local Government Elections, the Partnership on Information Integrity in Elections (PIIE) brings together the organisations that previously operated independently into a single coordinated coalition. This is new and is worth understanding clearly.

Before 2026, MMA / Real411 and Africa Check operated in parallel -- both involved in electoral information integrity, but through different mechanisms and different platform relationships. In 2024, the Atlantic Council's Digital Forensic Research Lab (DFRLab) documented them as separate activities. By 2026, they share a formal forum under PIIE leadership.

What We Know and What We Do Not

Real Safeguards. Real Questions.

Real411 has accountability mechanisms that are documented and real. Listing them matters because the strongest version of this investigation acknowledges what is in place before identifying what those mechanisms cannot answer.

No Binding Powers
Confirmed safeguard

Real411's own documentation states the Digital Complaints Committee has no legally binding authority. It refers and recommends. It cannot force any platform, authority or person to act.

Public Archive
Confirmed safeguard

Every complaint outcome is published and searchable. This makes it possible, in principle, to audit individual decisions for patterns of viewpoint bias -- if someone does the analysis.

Right of Appeal
Confirmed safeguard

Complainants and subjects can challenge decisions and request re-reviews. Appeals are overseen by Justice Zak Yacoob or equivalent senior legal figures.

Freedom of Expression Criteria
Confirmed safeguard

Real411's assessment explicitly considers satire, journalism and public interest as grounds that may justify content staying up, even if the content initially looks like a breach.

Those safeguards are real. Here is what they do not resolve:

  • 01
    The full text of the 2026 Framework. The cooperation agreement between MMA, the IEC, Meta, Google and TikTok has not been published. Moxii Africa's own page simultaneously says it is signed and that it is still under negotiation. Two PAIA requests have been filed. Without the document, the legal character of the "prioritised escalation" cannot be assessed.
  • 02
    Platform escalation outcomes. What proportion of Real411's platform referrals resulted in content removal versus warning versus delisting versus no action? That data has not been published. Without it, the real-world effect on what South Africans can read online cannot be measured.
  • 03
    Complaint distribution by political target. If the public archive were coded by the political affiliation or viewpoint of the subject of each complaint -- across all parties, movements and actors -- viewpoint asymmetry in referrals could be tested empirically. That analysis has not been published. It would either confirm or refute the suppression hypothesis more reliably than any inference from funding relationships.
  • 04
    Reviewer identities and conflict-of-interest declarations. The 30-plus volunteers are not publicly identified. Their institutional affiliations and any declarations of interest are unknown. This matters because the "any three claim the complaint" model provides no documented safeguard against all active reviewers sharing a common institutional background.
  • 05
    Trusted-flagger submission statistics. MMA's 2024 election report confirmed Meta created a trusted-flagger-equivalent profile for MMA and the IEC -- a faster, prioritised reporting channel. The number of submissions made through that channel and the proportion that resulted in platform action has not been published.
  • 06
    The MMA trust deed. Because MMA is a trust and not a company, its governance records sit at the Master of the High Court rather than at the CIPC (Companies and Intellectual Property Commission). Trustee identities and governance structures require a formal records access request to the Johannesburg Master's Office.
  • 07
    Funding earmarked for Real411 specifically. All identified grant records describe general operating support or broad media-monitoring purposes. None are itemised as specifically funding Real411 operations. Distinguishing MMA general institutional support from funds directed at the Real411 platform would allow more precise analysis.
