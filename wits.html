01 — Executive summary
Ten power systems. One campus. Zero oversight.
The University of the Witwatersrand (Wits) in Johannesburg is the physical convergence point of at least ten distinct international power systems operating simultaneously under one governance structure. United States technology capital (IBM's R700 million research lab). Open Society Foundations philanthropy ($3.9 million across 16 grants, plus Africa Check, the Centre for Applied Legal Studies, and Africa Is a Country funding). Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation health funding ($80 million in 2024-2025 alone). African National Congress bureaucratic training. Facebook content moderation authority over 350 million users. Journalist training for every major South African newsroom. Post-colonial theory production feeding the global reparations movement. US diplomatic engagement. Swedish university partnerships. German socialist foundation legal funding.
Each system operates independently. Each has its own funding logic, institutional objectives, and reporting structures. But they all operate inside the same campus, governed by the same Council, producing outputs that reinforce each other. The money flows in from New York, Seattle, Washington, Berlin, Ottawa, and Stockholm. What flows out shapes what a third of a billion people are allowed to think about race, identity, sovereignty, and power. The output does not stop at Africa's borders. Africa Check rulings are cited by CNN, PBS, BBC, Snopes, and Wikipedia. Achille Mbembe's 81,780 academic citations are overwhelmingly in Western journals. Herman Wasserman defines "disinformation" for Routledge handbooks read in every English-speaking university on earth. The architecture is in Johannesburg. The product is exported worldwide.

The person who governed Wits at the highest level (Isaac Shongwe, Chair of Council from 2019 to 2026) was simultaneously the past chairman of the Open Society Foundation for South Africa. 05B — The media arm
Africa Is a Country: the content pipeline that runs through Wits
Africa Is a Country is not a peripheral blog. With over 500 contributors, it is one of the leading intellectual voices in the African online media sphere. It was founded by Sean Jacobs, Associate Professor of International Affairs at The New School in New York. It is funded by the Open Society Foundations and the Shuttleworth Foundation. And its intellectual, personal, and institutional connections to Wits are so dense that the two are functionally part of the same ecosystem.
The founder
Sean Jacobs and the Wits web
Jacobs co-authored "Apartheid Israel: The Politics of an Analogy" with Achille Mbembe (Wits). His book "Media in Postapartheid South Africa" was published by Wits University Press. He and Herman Wasserman have been "close friends for over 25 years" (Jacobs' own words at the 2019 South African Communication Association keynote).
At that same keynote, Jacobs described Africa Is a Country's framework as explicitly Gramscian: "to win, in Antonio Gramsci's terms, the war of position." He cited Mbembe's concept of the "negative moment" as foundational to the platform's editorial direction. His Ford Foundation #AfricaNoFilter Fellowship connects to the same funding ecosystem. The personal, intellectual, and publishing connections between Jacobs, Mbembe, Wasserman, and Wits University Press form a closed loop.
The NGO vehicle
Jacobin Foundation 501(c)(3)
Since December 2016, Africa Is a Country has operated through the Jacobin Foundation, a US 501(c)(3) nonprofit registered at 388 Atlantic Avenue, Brooklyn, New York (EIN 46-4332395). Jacobin magazine describes itself as "the leading intellectual voice of the American left."
This means tax-deductible donations to "support African media" flow through a US socialist magazine's nonprofit entity. The Jacobin Foundation also publishes content produced by the Rosa Luxemburg Stiftung (German government socialist foundation, EUR 79 million). The funding chain: German government taxes fund the Rosa Luxemburg Stiftung, which produces content published by Jacobin, which hosts the nonprofit entity through which Africa Is a Country receives donations, which is also funded by the Open Society Foundations, which funds operations at Wits, which publishes Jacobs' books and houses Mbembe. Every link is disclosed. Nobody has connected them.

Progressive International was co-founded by Yanis Varoufakis and the Sanders Institute. Its council includes Jeremy Corbyn, Slavoj Zizek, Noam Chomsky, Naomi Klein, and Achille Mbembe. 08 — Strategic importance of location
Seven conditions that exist nowhere else on earth
The architecture is located in South Africa because it cannot function in the United States or the European Union. Seven conditions converge only at Wits.
Condition 01
Jurisdictional immunity
No Digital Services Act. No Congressional oversight. No Freedom of Information Act. No transparency obligations on content moderation.
If Africa Check operated from the European Union, it would face Digital Services Act transparency reports, independent audits, and mandatory methodology disclosure. From Braamfontein, it faces none of this. 350 million users are content-moderated from a jurisdiction with zero institutional capacity to audit what it does.
Condition 02
Moral inversion shield
Anti-apartheid institutional history transforms Western-funded operations into "African voices."
The same architecture operating from Washington would be immediately recognisable as Western influence. From Wits, it becomes "local knowledge production" and "decolonised scholarship." Every critique can be reframed as neo-colonial interference. The geographic location is the legitimacy laundering mechanism.
Condition 03
Cost multiplication
Every dollar invested buys five to eight times the institutional output.
$80 million from the Gates Foundation funds 47 health research projects. The same amount would fund perhaps eight in the United States. R700 million from IBM builds a world-class research lab. That sum would barely lease floor space in Manhattan. Maximum institutional output per dollar invested.
Condition 04
University immunity structure
Academic freedom protections create three layers of institutional camouflage.
Criticising Africa Check becomes criticising Wits Journalism. Criticising Wits Journalism becomes criticising Wits University. Criticising Wits University becomes criticising academic freedom. Criticising academic freedom at an anti-apartheid university becomes defending apartheid. Each layer reframes the criticism before it reaches the actual question.
Condition 05
Revolving door invisibility
Geographic distance is camouflage. Nobody in Washington tracks Braamfontein.
Gaspard's move from US Ambassador to Open Society President is tracked by the Washington press corps. His appearance on a panel at Wits is invisible to that same press corps. Shongwe's twelve simultaneous positions are all publicly disclosed across profiles in six countries. No single jurisdiction, no single press corps, and no single regulatory body sees the full picture because the picture spans continents.
Condition 06
Zero domestic scrutiny
The ANC government has no incentive to investigate. Wits trains its bureaucrats.
The School of Governance head sits on Ramaphosa's Economic Advisory Council. Africa Check suppresses content that embarrasses the ANC narrative on farm attacks and racial policy. The Centre for Applied Legal Studies does ANC-aligned constitutional advocacy. There is no opposition party, no media outlet, and no regulatory body in South Africa with both the capacity and the will to investigate this convergence.
